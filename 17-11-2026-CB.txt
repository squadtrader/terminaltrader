================================================================================

Neither release is likely to move the New Zealand dollar much on its own. The Financial Stability Report assesses risk rather than signalling policy, and the food price data is only a small input into quarterly inflation. The RBNZ’s warning that recent cost pressures and higher interest rates could reverse the easing in domestic stress points in a cautious direction. Its list of global risks, from Middle East energy disruption to an AI valuation correction, reads like a list of the shocks that tend to hurt risk-sensitive currencies like the kiwi.

---

Coming up later from neighbouring Australia:

---

New Zealand food prices fell in October. Meanwhile the RBNZ says the financial system is in good shape, but warns that Middle East conflict, high bond yields and stretched AI valuations could combine into a more severe shock.

Summary:

New Zealand food prices fell 0.4% in October from September, Statistics New Zealand data showed on Tuesday. The annual rate edged down to 2.3% from 2.4% in September. Separately, the Reserve Bank of New Zealand said in its latest Financial Stability Report that the country’s financial system remains resilient, even as global risks have continued to build.

Food prices

Food accounts for nearly a fifth of New Zealand’s consumer price index, so the monthly fall is a modestly softer input ahead of the next quarterly inflation release. The annual rate of 2.3% shows food prices are still rising year on year, only at a slightly slower pace than the month before.

Financial Stability Report

The RBNZ said global financial stability risks have increased, raising the chance of a significant shock reaching New Zealand. Conflict in the Middle East continues to disrupt energy and trade flows. Higher government bond yields are adding pressure on heavily indebted countries, and valuations of AI-related firms remain stretched despite recent falls. The central bank warned that growing financial links between AI companies could amplify any further correction. It also warned that these weak points could interact: higher energy costs would feed into inflation and bond yields, and from there into asset prices.

At home, the bank said financial stress has generally eased from last year’s peak but remains uneven. Smaller businesses and sectors that rely on discretionary household spending are under the most pressure. It cautioned that recent cost pressures and higher interest rates could slow or reverse the improvement. The housing market has softened, particularly in Auckland and Wellington, and modest growth in mortgage lending has limited the build-up of housing risk.

Banks hold strong capital and liquidity despite some squeeze on lending margins and profitability. A joint stress test with Australia’s prudential regulator found that the four largest banks could keep lending through a severe downturn. Life and health insurers generally needed several severe shocks before they breached minimum requirements.

The report also flagged frontier AI models as a fast-growing cyber threat, because they speed up attacks and shrink the time available to fix software weaknesses. Governor Anna Breman urged institutions to keep strengthening their cyber defences and their ability to recover quickly from disruptions.

Bullock’s comment pushes back modestly against market pricing that has all but ruled out a December hike. It could offer the Australian dollar some support after it fell this week to its lowest level since late January. One line on a panel is unlikely to shift pricing on its own, so the minutes are the bigger test. Any sign that the majority on the board saw a real chance of further tightening would firm up the hawkish reading of Bullock’s remarks. Minutes preview here:

-

Bullock says inflation risks are still tilted higher. That reminds markets that a split board doesn’t mean the RBA is done, just as the minutes are due to show how close the debate was.

Summary:

Reserve Bank of Australia Governor Michele Bullock said on Tuesday that the risks to inflation remain tilted to the upside, Reuters reported. Her remarks come shortly before the release of minutes from the central bank’s November meeting.

Speaking on a panel at the ASIC Annual Forum in Sydney, Bullock said the board’s current view is that inflation risks are still skewed higher. The comment was brief, but its timing matters. Since the November decision, markets have priced out nearly all chance of a December rate rise. Her words suggest the board is not yet ready to declare its tightening campaign over.

The RBA lifted its cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.85% on 3 November, its fifth increase this year and the highest level since 2008. The vote was unusually split: six board members backed the hike and three preferred to hold at 4.60%. That division led many analysts to conclude the bank is close to the end of its cycle.

The minutes, due at 11.30am Sydney time (0030 GMT, 7.30pm ET on Monday), should shed more light on that debate. Analysts will look at how the board weighed inflation risks against a slowing economy, whether the three dissenters judged policy already restrictive enough, and how high the bar now is for a further move. The RBA’s own assessment puts the cash rate slightly above the top of its range of neutral rate estimates. Its forecasts have inflation staying above 3% until late 2027.

Bullock’s warning about upside risks sits alongside that restrictive stance. The board may judge that rates are high enough for now, while still wanting to keep the option of another hike open if inflation does not ease as expected.

The domestic calendar is busy for the rest of the week. Third-quarter wage price data is due on Wednesday and October employment figures on Thursday, when unemployment is expected to hold at 4.7%. Firm readings on either would test the market’s conviction that the RBA has finished hiking.

TITRE : RBA minutes: 6 members saw rates “not clearly sufficient”, 3 warned of sharper downturn
DATE  : 17-11-2026 00:36 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/rba-minutes-6-members-saw-rates-not-clearly-sufficient-3-warned-of-sharper-downturn/
================================================================================

On balance the minutes lean hawkish, unsurpisngly. The majority’s view that 4.85% is “not clearly sufficient” sits awkwardly with market pricing that had all but ruled out further hikes, and it backs up Bullock’s comment this morning about upside risks. The minority’s warning of a sharper downturn sets a high bar for a December move, though. This week’s wage and jobs data, and the October CPI jump the staff flagged in advance, matter more than usual. For the Australian dollar, near 0.6730, the minutes are mildly supportive but unlikely to change the trend while policy diverges from the Fed. The minutes treat oil as the swing factor: if prices hold rather than ease by around US$30 a barrel as assumed, the inflation case for another hike strengthens.

---

Earlier

---

The RBA majority hiked because 4.85% might not be enough, and the minority held back because the economy might already be cracking. That leaves the next move hostage to this week’s jobs data and the oil price.

Summary:

Most Reserve Bank of Australia board members judged that the cash rate was “not clearly sufficient” to bring inflation under control when they voted to raise it to 4.85% this month, according to minutes of the November meeting released on Tuesday. A three-member minority warned the economy might be close to a materially sharper downturn.

The minutes lay bare a board divided over the balance of risks. The six members who backed the 25 basis point increase, the fifth hike of the year, said forward-looking inflation pressures had risen and the risks to the outlook remained to the upside. They judged that the danger of leaving policy insufficiently restrictive, and allowing inflation expectations to drift higher, outweighed the risk of tightening too much.

The majority pointed to three signs that domestic capacity pressures were playing a bigger role than previously thought: strong inflation in market services, persistent growth in unit labour costs, and broad-based cost pressures reported by business contacts. Staff have revised up their estimate of the unemployment rate consistent with stable inflation, which implies the labour market was tighter in 2026 than they had assessed. Members also noted that the neutral interest rate had likely risen, which makes any given cash rate less restrictive than before.

The three dissenters preferred to hold at 4.60%. They argued it was prudent to wait and assess the 100 basis points of tightening already delivered this year. They placed more weight on downside risks to growth, citing a half-percentage-point rise in unemployment over six months, the housing downturn and fragile sentiment toward investment in data centres.

The Middle East conflict featured heavily. Members noted that crude and refined fuel prices had risen 20% to 25% since August. The forecasts assume oil prices fall by around US$30 a barrel over two years, an assumption that requires some resolution of the conflict and leaves significant upside risk if prices stay high. Staff expect headline inflation to jump in October as last year’s energy subsidies drop out.

The board offered no explicit guidance, saying only that it will remain attentive to the data and do what is necessary to return inflation to target. Earlier on Tuesday, Governor Michele Bullock said inflation risks are still skewed to the upside. Wage data on Wednesday and October jobs figures on Thursday are the next tests.
