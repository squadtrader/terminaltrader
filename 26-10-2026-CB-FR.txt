DATE  : 26-10-2026 00:43 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/ecb-set-to-hold-at-2-50-as-morgan-stanley-sees-december-as-natural-moment-for-next-hike/
================================================================================

With a hold almost fully priced, the euro’s reaction will turn on whether Lagarde validates December hike expectations or keeps full optionality, and a noncommittal message could see some tightening priced out and weigh on the single currency. The Fed decision a day earlier sets the backdrop for EUR/USD, so a hawkish Fed followed by a cautious ECB would be the most euro-negative combination. Higher oil linked to the Middle East conflict remains the main upside risk to eurozone inflation and to hike pricing. French government bonds are exposed to any sign that the ECB sees the recent rise in yields as a concern rather than welcome tightening, and Friday’s flash inflation and GDP data could quickly reprice December either way.

---

Earlier:

---

The ECB looks set to take a breather this week, letting higher bond yields do some of its work, while December shapes up as the meeting where the next hike gets decided.

Summary:

The European Central Bank is widely expected to keep its deposit rate unchanged at 2.50% on Thursday, pausing after September’s rate increase as policymakers weigh an uncertain economic outlook against inflation that is still running well above target.

Morgan Stanley analysts said in a note that there is so far no sign of significant indirect or second-round effects from the latest energy shock on inflation, and that the recent rise in long-term bond yields is already providing a degree of additional tightening. Together, those factors remove any urgency to deliver another hike this week, the bank said, adding that December, when policymakers will have fresh data and updated staff projections, looks like the more natural moment to shift the policy stance.

That view is widely shared. In a Reuters poll conducted earlier this month, 70 of 73 economists expected the ECB to hold in October, while 64 of 73 saw a further 25 basis point increase in December, a sharp reversal from the previous survey, when most thought the tightening cycle was over.

The jump in sovereign bond yields, particularly in France amid concerns over its debt and budget, is likely to be on policymakers’ minds. ING economists argue that the French fiscal crisis and higher yields are already tightening financial conditions, making a hold very likely and pushing any further decision to December. Commerzbank expects the deposit rate to rise to 2.75% in December and then stay there throughout 2027, pointing to an anticipated decline in oil prices and the dampening effect of higher yields as reasons against further moves.

Investors will focus on President Christine Lagarde’s press conference for any signals on the prospect of further rate increases in the coming months. The decision is due at 13:15 GMT (09:15 ET), with the press conference following at 13:45 GMT (09:45 ET).

The data calendar is busy. Germany’s Ifo business survey for October is out on Monday, the GfK consumer confidence survey follows on Wednesday, and eurozone business and consumer surveys are due on Thursday. On Friday, provisional third-quarter eurozone GDP figures will be released alongside flash October inflation data from individual countries, including France and Germany, offering an early read on whether the case for a December hike is strengthening.

TITRE : BOJ to hold at 1.25%, but Ueda signals further hikes toward neutral
DATE  : 26-10-2026 01:54 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/boj-to-hold-at-1-25-but-ueda-signals-further-hikes-toward-neutral/
================================================================================

With a hold widely expected, yen and JGB moves are likely to hinge on the vote and Ueda’s guidance. Hawkish dissent from Takata or Tamura, or language pointing to the next meeting, would probably bring December pricing forward and offer the yen some support. A unanimous hold paired with cautious messaging could see hike expectations drift toward January, keeping the yen soft. Front-end JGB yields remain the most sensitive gauge of shifts in timing, and Friday’s Tokyo CPI print lands on the same day as the decision, adding to the potential for volatility.

---

Earlier:

---

Ueda is in no rush to hike again this week, but he has made clear the BOJ is not done, with the debate now about whether the next move comes in December or January.

Summary:

The Bank of Japan is expected to leave its policy rate unchanged at 1.25% at its October 29-30 meeting, with Governor Kazuo Ueda signalling there is no need for back-to-back increases but that rates will have to rise further toward a neutral level, according to Mace News.

The board raised the rate from 1% in a 7-2 vote in September. Since then, Ueda has said that oversized or consecutive hikes are typically reserved for situations where inflation is rising rapidly beyond target, and that the bank is making pre-emptive adjustments to avoid being forced into abrupt tightening later. Speaking in Bangkok on October 16, he said that with underlying inflation now close to 2%, the bank can no longer tolerate further increases and its main goal is to stabilise inflation around that level. Board member Junko Koeda also argued last week that the bank should not become overly cautious about raising rates.

Mace News sees the most likely path as a hike to 1.50% in December or January, followed by a rise to 1.75% in March or April. That would continue a faster pattern of tightening every three to four months.

The board is broadly divided into four camps. Hawks Hajime Takata and Naoki Tamura may start pushing for the next increase on Friday, setting the stage for possible action at the December 17-18 meeting. At the dovish end, Toichiro Asada and Ayano Sato have dissented against recent hikes.

On the data front, Tokyo’s October CPI is forecast to show inflation at 2.7% to 2.8% across all three key measures, partly reflecting the fading impact of local fiscal support. September jobs, industrial production and retail sales figures are also due.

TITRE : Major central banks take centre stage this week but will there be any surprises?
DATE  : 26-10-2026 05:06 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/major-central-banks-take-centre-stage-this-week-but-will-there-be-any-surprises/
================================================================================

The central bank bonanza is back in town this week, with five big names lining up to announce their latest monetary policy decisions. After all the recent drama involving oil prices, bond yields, and the stock market, perhaps it is time for central bankers to have their say again.

Of note, we will have the RBNZ, BOC, Fed, ECB, and BOJ all on the agenda this week. And looking to market pricing, traders seem to have a pretty good idea of what to expect already from the decisions to come. So, the only question is will there be any surprises? Let's see.

TheRBNZwill kick things off on Wednesday with traders fully pricing in a 25 bps rate hike. That leaves little suspense surrounding the decision itself, although traders will be watching for clues on whether policymakers are prepared to tighten further in December.

Then, theBOCwill follow later in the day with markets pricing in roughly 83% odds of a 25 bps rate hike. The pricing reflects relatively strong conviction with just a bit of room for a potential surprise. But all else being equal, the central bank should deliver as expected this week. Perhaps the bigger question is whether another rate hike will be needed as policymakers continue to wrestle with inflation risks. So, that will be the key risk trigger for the loonie reaction in the aftermath.

After that, we will have theFedto finish off the day with traders assigning around 91% odds of no change in interest rates. As such, the decision itself won't be the main talking point here. Instead, it will all rest on Fed chair Warsh's pess conference. His remarks will be closely scrutinised, especially with rising oil prices complicating the inflation outlook while recent weakness in the stock market raises fresh concerns about growth.

And on Thursday, it is then on theECBto take the wheel with markets pricing in roughly 90% odds of a hold. But with energy-driven inflation pressures building and European growth showing signs of weakness, ECB president Lagarde will have a lot to address with her press conference. In particular, traders will be looking for clues on December, where expectations for another rate hike have been building.

Finally, theBOJwill round things off on Friday with traders seeing roughly 98% odds of no change in interest rates. But with the yen still struggling and underlying inflation holding near 2%, the focus will be on whether policymakers offer any stronger signals about a potential move in December. BOJ governor Ueda's press conference is likely to steer clear of any pre-commitment though, so there might not be much to the key risk event here; all else being equal.

All things considered, I wouldn't expect the interest rate decisions alone to deliver much drama this week. As we've seen time and again, it is often what central bankers say about their next move that ends up stirring markets the most. So, that is what I'll be watching more closely with the meeting decisions and press conferences this week.
