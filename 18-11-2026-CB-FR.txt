TITRE : Australian wages jump 1.1%, strongest since 2023, but real pay falls for a fourth quarter
DATE  : 18-11-2026 00:38 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/australian-wages-beat-at-1-1-as-award-rise-hands-rba-hawks-a-fresh-argument/
================================================================================

The headline beat should support the Australian dollar and short-dated yields at the margin, as traders reassess how firmly they had ruled out another RBA hike. The detail limits the hawkish case, though. Private sector strength came from a one-off award decision, public sector pay is doing much of the work, and real wages are still falling, so it’s hard to argue wages are driving inflation. That mix makes a large repricing of December less likely. For AUD/USD, rate expectations at home are still competing with a Fed on a hiking path.

---

Preview here:

---

Australian pay growth was the strongest in nearly three years, but it still didn’t keep up with prices, which gives both camps on the RBA board something to work with.

Summary:

Australian wages rose 1.1% in the September quarter, the strongest quarterly gain since late 2023 and ahead of the 1.0% economists had expected, Australian Bureau of Statistics data showed on Wednesday. Annual growth picked up to 3.5% from 3.2%.

The Fair Work Commission’s larger than expected 4.75% increase in award wages, which mostly flows through in the September quarter, drove the lift in private sector pay. The public sector was stronger again. Public wages rose 1.3% in the quarter, driven by frontline workers, and have now outpaced private sector wages for seven consecutive quarters.

Workers are still falling behind. Real wages fell in the quarter and were down 0.4% over the year, the fourth straight quarter of negative annual growth, as inflation continued to run ahead of pay.

The data gives the Reserve Bank of Australia’s hawks more to work with. The bank lifted the cash rate to 4.85% earlier this month, its fifth hike this year and an 18-year high, on a split 6-3 vote. Minutes released on Tuesday showed the majority judged that rates were not clearly sufficient to bring inflation under control, and Governor Michele Bullock said the same day that inflation risks remain skewed to the upside. A wage print that beats forecasts feeds into services inflation, the part of the price basket the RBA has found hardest to bring down.

The doves have their own arguments in the detail. The private sector lift came from a one-off award decision rather than a tight labour market, and much of the strength sits in public sector pay set outside market pressures. Falling real wages also suggest pay is catching up with inflation rather than driving it, with unemployment at 4.7% and job advertisements declining.

Markets had all but priced out a December hike before the release. This result makes that call look less certain, but it probably does not overturn it. The monthly inflation data due before the December meeting will carry more weight. If prices also run hot, the RBA debate could shift from whether rates have peaked to whether one more hike is needed.

TITRE : Ueda keeps December hike in play as BOJ wants inflation anchored around 2%
DATE  : 18-11-2026 01:23 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/ueda-keeps-december-hike-in-play-as-boj-wants-inflation-anchored-around-2/
================================================================================

Ueda’s remarks reinforce December hike expectations without adding a fresh hawkish trigger, so the yen reaction is likely to be modest. His point that financial conditions remain accommodative supports the view that the BOJ has more tightening to do beyond December, which should keep upward pressure on short-end JGB yields. The rate gap with a Fed on a hiking path still dominates USD/JPY, which is holding near 160 where Tokyo has warned about intervention. Oil remains the wildcard: higher energy costs from the Iran war would push import prices and underlying inflation up, strengthening Ueda’s case.

---

Earlier:

---

Ueda gave the market more of the same, more hikes ahead and no panic over the food tax cut, and that is exactly what a central bank heading towards December wants to say.

Summary:

Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda said on Wednesday that the central bank will keep raising its policy rate in line with economic, price and financial developments, reaffirming the tightening path ahead of next month’s policy meeting.

Ueda said it was important to see inflation anchored around the bank’s 2% target, and noted that financial conditions remain accommodative. That suggests he sees room to lift rates further without choking off growth. His comments follow the bank’s October decision to hold its policy rate at 1.25%, when he declared that underlying inflation had reached 2% and signalled strong prospects for a hike in December.

A large part of his remarks addressed the government’s planned cut to the consumption tax on food. Ueda said the impact of the tax cut was likely to stay limited, but described its effect on the economy and on underlying inflation as highly uncertain for now. He said the bank would assess how the cut feeds through to underlying inflation and set policy accordingly.

The distinction matters for markets. A cut to the food consumption tax would mechanically lower headline inflation when it takes effect, which could make price data look softer than the underlying trend. Ueda’s focus on underlying inflation signals that the Bank of Japan is unlikely to treat a tax-driven drop in prices as a reason to stop raising rates. At the same time, his caution about the uncertainty leaves him room to adjust if the cut boosts spending more than expected.

The comments come after stronger than expected export data earlier on Wednesday, which showed shipments up 20.5% in October and a narrower than forecast trade deficit. Third-quarter GDP contracted slightly, but the bank has pointed to firm wage growth as the stronger signal.

For the yen, Ueda’s remarks reinforce expectations of a December hike without adding a new hawkish signal. With the Federal Reserve also on a hiking path, the rate gap is likely to keep USD/JPY close to the 160 level until the Bank of Japan moves faster or the Fed pauses. Friday’s national inflation data is the next test.
