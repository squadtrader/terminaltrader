Economists agree the RBA will hike again, but the split over what comes next, along with a hike already in the price, means the guidance will matter more than the move for the Australian dollar.

Summary:

The Reserve Bank of Australia is widely expected to raise its cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.85% on Tuesday. That would be its fifth increase this year and take the rate to its highest level since 2008. Economists agree on the move but are split on what comes after it.

The decision is due at 2:30 p.m. AEDT (0330 GMT), with Governor Michele Bullock’s press conference to follow. The bank lifted the cash rate to 4.60% in September, and hawkish remarks from senior officials since then have pushed forecasters toward another rise. Underlying inflation has also run hotter than the RBA projected, with the trimmed mean rising 1.0% in the September quarter against the bank’s 0.8% forecast.

He argues that holding steady could damage its credibility and further entrench higher inflation expectations. That marks a shift for Oliver, who had earlier not expected a November hike.

The backdrop complicates the outlook. House prices fell for a seventh consecutive month in October, adding to pressure on borrowers as earlier rate rises continue to feed through. Any acknowledgment of that strain in Tuesday’s statement would be read as a signal the RBA is nearing the top of its cycle.

For the Australian dollar, the bar for a strong rally looks high. Interbank futures had priced a hike as likely, leaving the currency more sensitive to the tone of the statement and Bullock’s comments than to the decision itself. A clear signal that further tightening is possible would offer support, while a message closer to AMP’s hike-and-hold view could see the currency give up ground.

TITRE : China’s PBOC lifts FX reserve ratio to 6%: what it means for the yuan, AUD
DATE  : 03-11-2026 00:56 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/china-s-pboc-lifts-fx-reserve-ratio-to-6-what-it-means-for-the-yuan-aud/
================================================================================

The move signals that Beijing is uncomfortable with the pace of the yuan’s recent strength. That gives USD/CNY and USD/CNH a floor near current levels rather than reversing the trend outright. Its direct liquidity effect is modest, so traders are likely to treat it as a warning shot, with the daily fixing the main tool to watch for follow-through. The signal reaches beyond China: a PBOC leaning against appreciation tends to temper gains in other Asian currencies that trade alongside the yuan, such as the Korean won and Taiwan dollar. It can also weigh at the margin on the Australian dollar, which often trades as a China proxy. The timing matters too, with the US dollar already softer on Iran diplomacy hopes. If the dollar weakens further, the PBOC may need stronger signals to hold the line.

---

Earlier:

---

China’s central bank is making dollars scarcer onshore to take some heat out of a strengthening yuan, a modest move in size but a clear signal of where Beijing wants the currency to go.

Summary:

China’s central bank will require banks to hold a larger share of their foreign currency deposits in reserve. The move is a technical tightening of onshore dollar liquidity that markets are likely to read as a signal that Beijing wants to slow the yuan’s rise.

The People’s Bank of China said it will raise the foreign exchange deposit reserve ratio for financial institutions by 2 percentage points, to 6% from 4%, from November 15. It described the step as a way to strengthen the management of foreign exchange liquidity in the banking system.

The ratio works much like the better-known reserve requirement on yuan deposits, but it applies to deposits held in foreign currencies, mostly US dollars. When the ratio rises, banks must park more of those dollars with the central bank, leaving fewer available to lend, invest or sell in the onshore market. With dollars harder to come by, they become relatively more expensive against the yuan, which takes some pressure off the Chinese currency’s appreciation.

The PBOC uses the tool in both directions. It raised the ratio twice in 2021, when the yuan was climbing strongly, and cut it in 2022 and again in 2023 as the currency weakened and the central bank sought to support it. The ratio had stood at 4% since the last cut, making this the first increase in several years.

The size of the move is modest. When the PBOC cut the ratio by the same 2 percentage points in 2022, ANZ estimated it would add around $20 billion of foreign currency to the market, which is small relative to China’s currency market. The signal, rather than the cash amount, is the main point.

Other indicators point the same way. On Monday, the PBOC set its daily USD/CNY reference rate at around 6.71, considerably weaker for the yuan than the roughly 6.66 that Reuters’ survey of models had expected. That suggests the central bank was already leaning against the currency’s strength. The PBOC has also said it aims to keep the yuan broadly stable and to guard against the risk of the exchange rate overshooting.

For traders, the practical takeaway is that Beijing is signalling a preference for slower, steadier yuan gains. It is not reversing the currency’s direction. The daily fixing will be the clearest guide to whether more steps follow, particularly if the US dollar weakens further.

The RBA hiked again as its inflation forecasts jumped, but a three-member dissent and a non-committal outlook suggest the board is closer to the top of the cycle than its forecasts alone imply.

Summary:

The Reserve Bank of Australia raised its cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.85% on Tuesday, its fifth increase this year and the highest level since 2008. A three-member dissent, however, signalled growing division over how much further policy needs to tighten.

Six members of the Monetary Policy Board voted for the hike, while three preferred to keep the rate at 4.60%. In its statement, the board said inflation remains elevated and broadly based, and that there is an increasing risk higher inflation expectations will become embedded. It judged that further tightening in financial conditions was warranted to support the return of inflation to target.

The accompanying Statement on Monetary Policy showed a material upgrade to the inflation outlook. Headline inflation is now forecast to peak at 4.5% in the December quarter, and both headline and trimmed mean inflation are expected to stay above 3% until late 2027, about three quarters longer than forecast in August. Inflation is not expected to return to the 2.5% midpoint of the target band until mid-2028. Headline inflation was 3.9% over the year to the September quarter, with trimmed mean at 3.7%.

The RBA attributed the revision to two factors. Fuel and other commodity prices have risen sharply since the Middle East conflict re-escalated, with Brent crude around US$115 a barrel at the time of the forecasts. Staff also now judge domestic capacity pressures to be tighter than previously thought, and lifted their estimate of the unemployment rate consistent with stable inflation to around 4.75%. The bank also noted that AI-related demand is pushing up prices for technology goods, and that some measures of inflation expectations have risen.

The economy is slowing under the weight of higher rates. House prices were almost 5% below their March peak in September, unemployment averaged 4.6% in the September quarter, and the jobless rate is forecast to rise to 5.1% by end-2028. Strong investment in data centres is partly offsetting weaker household spending and housing activity.

The forecasts assume the cash rate rises by around 35 basis points by mid-2027 in line with market pricing, a path up to 50 basis points higher than in August. On guidance, though, the board said only that it will be attentive to the data and will do what is necessary to bring inflation back to target. That stops short of an explicit commitment to further hikes.

The outcome matched expectations from HSBC and AMP, while the split vote and cautious guidance sit closer to AMP’s hike-and-hold view than to ANZ’s warning of a move to 5.10%.

Earlier:

Bullock’s comments sent a mixed signal for the Australian dollar and the front end of the curve. Her warning that inflation is largely locked in for the next year, and that unemployment must rise further, underlines the RBA’s resolve. Her account of the dissent, though, suggests a significant minority is already wary of overtightening. That balance supports the view that further hikes remain possible but are far from assured, limiting the scope for a strong rebound in the AUD after its post-decision dip. For Australian equities, the explicit acceptance of weaker growth and higher unemployment adds to pressure on rate-sensitive sectors such as banks, retailers and property, which the RBA itself identified as under strain.

--

Bullock made the case for accepting some economic pain now to stop inflation becoming entrenched, but her description of the dissent shows a board increasingly divided over how far to push.

Summary:

Reserve Bank of Australia Governor Michele Bullock said on Tuesday that the central bank’s latest rate rise was needed to bring inflation down, and that the economy will have to endure subdued growth and higher unemployment to get there.

Speaking at her press conference after the board lifted the cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.85%, Bullock said the tightening was aimed at domestic capacity pressures. She argued that accepting some pain now would help prevent further pain down the track. Growth will need to stay subdued and unemployment to rise further, she said.

Bullock was frank about the near-term inflation outlook. She said inflation is largely baked in for the next year and described that as not a good situation to be in. The comments align with the RBA’s updated forecasts, released alongside the decision, which see headline inflation peaking at 4.5% in the December quarter and both headline and trimmed mean inflation staying above 3% until late 2027.

The governor also shed light on the board’s division. Six members voted for the hike and three preferred to hold at 4.60%. Bullock said those favouring a hold saw greater downside risks to the economy and felt the board should wait a little longer for more data before acting.

On the outlook, Bullock said she does not know what the next move in rates will be and that the board is not offering forward guidance. She added that it would probably be sensible to wait now and assess incoming data, comments likely to reinforce speculation that Tuesday’s increase could be followed by a pause.

The RBA publishes its votes without attributing them to individual members, so the identities of the dissenters have not been disclosed.

The hike, the RBA’s fifth this year, takes the cash rate to its highest level since 2008. In its statement, the board said it would remain attentive to the data and do what is necessary to return inflation to target, without explicitly committing to further increases. The Australian dollar fell after the decision, which markets had widely expected, amid some speculation that the split vote could mean this is the last hike of the cycle.

Bullock also expressed confidence that the tightening will have the intended effect, saying monetary policy works but takes time to feed through to the economy. She noted that unemployment remains relatively low and that jobs growth is continuing, adding that she does not see the economy cracking at this point.
