The comments are likely to firm pricing for a December hike, which would mark a turn from the lowest policy rate among major central banks and could lend support to the Swiss franc. With fuel and heating costs driving the jump, the oil price is now a direct input to Swiss rate expectations, tying SNB policy more closely to developments in the Middle East. A franc-supportive shift would also reduce one of the main channels through which the SNB has historically leaned against imported inflation. ---

Swiss inflation is still inside the SNB’s target, but the speed of the move has caught the bank’s attention, and its chairman wants markets to know it.

Summary:

Swiss National Bank Chairman Martin Schlegel said the central bank was caught off guard by the recent jump in Swiss inflation, and signalled it is ready to adjust policy if price stability comes under threat, according to Reuters, citing an interview with newspaper Handelszeitung published on Thursday.

Swiss consumer prices rose 1.5% in Octoberfrom a year earlier, government data showed on Tuesday, the highest rate in nearly three years. Schlegel said the reading came in above even the SNB’s own expectations and that inflation had risen since the bank’s last policy assessment.

He attributed the increase mainly to higher fuel and heating costs, though prices for clothing, food and footwear also climbed during the month.

The SNB targets annual inflation of between 0% and 2%, so October’s reading remains inside its range. The concern is less the level than the direction and breadth of price pressures. Schlegel said the bank would monitor the situation closely, paying particular attention to whether higher prices begin feeding into other parts of the economy, such as wages and rents.

So far, he said, there had been no significant evidence of those so-called second-round effects, though he could not rule them out in future. He added that the SNB would not hesitate to change its monetary policy should price stability be put at risk.

The remarks carry weight because of where Swiss policy currently sits. The SNB’s policy rate of 0% is the lowest among major central banks, and the October data had already led economists to raise expectations that the bank could lift rates at its next meeting.

Schlegel’s comments stopped short of committing to a move, but his acknowledgement that inflation has surprised the bank, combined with an explicit readiness to act, is likely to reinforce that view. The SNB’s next policy decision is due on December 10.

DATE  : 04-11-2026 23:58 UTC

The minutes add a hawkish edge to the yen outlook, with talk of a faster pace and getting closer to neutral “relatively soon” supporting pricing for a December move. Against that, one member’s view that market expectations are running ahead of the economy may cap how far rate pricing extends, particularly at the longer end of the JGB curve. Oil is now an explicit driver of BoJ thinking, so renewed crude strength from the Middle East is likely to feed directly into Japanese rate expectations. The Cabinet Office’s call for caution is a reminder that political resistance to rapid tightening remains a factor.

-

The BoJ board in September was arguing over how fast to go, not whether to keep going, with oil doing much of the persuading.

Summary:

Bank of Japan policymakers agreed at their September meeting that the central bank should keep raising interest rates, with some members arguing for a faster pace if prices threaten to overshoot, according to minutes released on Thursday that also showed the government urging caution over the cumulative effect of past hikes.

At the September 17 and 18 meeting, the BoJ raised its policy rate by 25 basis points to 1.25%, the highest since 1995, in a 7-2 vote. The minutes show members agreed it was appropriate to keep lifting rates and adjusting the degree of monetary accommodation, given that underlying inflation was approaching 2% and financial conditions remained accommodative.

Several members pointed to a shift in the phase of policy. One said the aim was no longer to push underlying inflation higher but to stabilise it around 2%. Another said the BoJ should show markets its determination to prevent prices overshooting by responding nimbly to overseas conditions. A third said the bank would need to speed up its rate hikes if signs of an upward deviation in prices emerged, while another favoured bringing rates closer to the neutral level relatively soon to leave room to move in either direction.

Not all were in a hurry. One member said underlying inflation was not accelerating fast enough to risk the bank falling behind the curve, so hasty action was unnecessary. The two dissenters, Toichiro Asada and Ayano Sato, preferred to hold at 1%. Asada argued that with core inflation below 2%, the economy could not necessarily be called strong, while Sato said economic and price trends had not substantially accelerated.

Oil featured heavily. One member noted crude prices had risen about 50% since July while the yen had appreciated about 5%, which would push up consumer prices considerably if sustained. A few members flagged the tail risk of physical shortages of crude, not just higher prices, citing the suspension of Saudi Arabia’s pipeline operations, unstable shipping through the Red Sea and falling reserves in the US and China.

Members also debated the neutral rate. One noted that a market gauge of the expected peak in rates had risen above 2%, while another said market expectations for hikes appeared somewhat ahead of the economy.

The government’s representatives struck a more cautious tone. The Cabinet Office said the BoJ should examine carefully the cumulative effects of past hikes, consider its neutral rate estimates and act appropriately in the event of excessive swings in the economy and markets.

Since the meeting, the BoJ held rates at 1.25% in October, while saying underlying inflation had reached levels consistent with its target. The minutes reinforce that the debate is over the pace of further tightening, not its direction, keeping attention on the December meeting.

With a hike almost fully priced, sterling’s reaction will hinge on the vote split and guidance, with a 7-2 outcome and cautious language the most likely route to a softer pound. The Bank of England is set to hike; what it says about the next one will matter more.

Summary:

The Bank of England is widely expected to raise interest rates on Thursday for the first time in this cycle, lifting Bank Rate by 25 basis points to 4% as energy costs push UK inflation further above target, with the decision due at 12:00 GMT (07:00 ET).

The move would follow a closely divided September meeting, when the Monetary Policy Committee held Bank Rate at 3.75% in a 6-3 vote, with three members already favouring a rise to 4%. Since then, the inflation picture has deteriorated. Consumer prices rose 3.5% in the year to September, up from 3.1% in August and higher than expected, driven largely by rising fuel costs.

Markets have almost fully priced in a hike, and research houses are close to unanimous. Goldman Sachs expects a 7-2 vote in favour, a split shared by much of the consensus, with the two likely dissenters preferring to hold. Some forecasters see a narrower margin.

The more important question for markets is what comes next. Money markets have priced in several further increases over the coming year, a path many economists consider too aggressive. The more common base case is one additional quarter-point rise early next year, taking Bank Rate to 4.25%, possibly in February, when inflation is projected to peak. Goldman expects the committee to signal readiness to respond if inflation pressure builds, but without a strong steer on future meetings.

This is also a forecast meeting, with the Bank publishing an updated Monetary Policy Report. Its new projections are likely to show a higher near-term inflation peak in the first half of 2027, with Goldman expecting it at above 4%. Oil and gas prices are now running above the adverse scenario the Bank set out in July, raising the risk that the Middle East conflict leaves a more lasting mark on UK inflation.

That is the central concern for the hawks. Underlying measures remain firm, with services inflation approaching 4%, and the key risk is that higher energy costs become embedded in wages and price-setting. Against that, wage growth has been described as soft and the consumer is under pressure, giving the doves grounds for caution.

For sterling and gilts, the guidance and vote split are likely to matter more than the hike itself. A cautious message alongside a widely expected increase could see markets pare back their more aggressive pricing, while any sign of a faster pace would reinforce it.

TITRE : Bank of England raises the Bank Rate to 4.00% as expected in a 9-0 vote
DATE  : 05-11-2026 12:00 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/bank-of-england-raises-the-bank-rate-to-4-00-as-expected-in-a-9-0-vote/
================================================================================

The Bank of England raised interest rates by 25 bps to 4.00% as widely expected, as policymakers become increasingly concerned about the inflationary consequences of the conflict in the Middle East. The decision was supported by an 8-1 majority, with one member voting for a larger 50 bps increase to 4.25%.

The BoE noted that UK CPI inflation rose to 3.5% in September and is now expected to approach 5% by mid-2027, reflecting the continued rise in global energy prices. Although the direct impact on household and business energy bills is clear, the Committee stressed that there is still limited evidence of significant second-round effects on wages and other prices.

Nevertheless, policymakers judged that the inflationary pressures building in the pipeline were sufficient to warrant a rate increase. Stronger-than-expected economic activity data in recent months also added to the case for tighter monetary policy.

The BoE stressed that monetary policy is not on a pre-set path and that decisions will be made on a meeting-by-meeting basis. The Committee will pay particular attention to underlying wage growth, businesses' pricing behaviour and the degree of economic slack to assess whether higher energy costs are spreading into broader inflation. This suggests policymakers want to retain flexibility rather than commit to a prolonged tightening cycle.

However, the comments in the minutes showed a relatively hawkish debate within the MPC. Lombardelli and Pill see a case for further rate increases in the near term, with Pill suggesting that a single 25 bps hike is unlikely to be sufficient. Greene also indicated that additional increases will probably be required, while Mann warned that the economy is now operating in a more challenging environment for second-round inflation effects.

On the other side, Taylor and Dhingra viewed today's increase primarily as insurance against worse inflation outcomes. Governor Bailey added that part of the recent rise in market interest-rate expectations reflects higher risk premia rather than expectations of further policy tightening, arguing that current monetary conditions are broadly appropriate.

The BoE raised its 2027 GDP growth forecast to 1.3%, from 1.1% previously, while warning that economic activity is likely to weaken over the coming quarters. Unemployment is now expected to reach 5.1% in 2028, compared with 5.2% in the previous projections, while private-sector regular wage growth is forecast to remain relatively resilient, reaching 3.5% in Q4 2027.

Overall, today's decision signals that the BoE is increasingly concerned about the risk of energy-driven inflation becoming entrenched, but remains cautious given the limited second-round effects. Nevertheless, traders added to BoE rate hike bets and are now pricing a 50% chance of another increase in December.
