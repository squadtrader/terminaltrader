JPMorgan has joined the RBA hike camp, betting that a central bank still worried about inflation will move again on Tuesday, even as the economy shows signs of strain.

Summary:

JPMorgan now expects the Reserve Bank of Australia to raise interest rates again this week, pointing to recent inflation data and comments from senior central bank officials as evidence that the board’s tightening cycle is not yet complete.

Ben Jarman, JPMorgan’s chief economist for Australia, said the RBA has consistently signalled that inflation is not yet under control and that it still has work to do. Market pricing puts the probability of a further increase in the official cash rate at about 90%, leaving little doubt about the expected outcome.

That tension was visible in Australian data released earlier on Monday. The S&P Global manufacturing survey showed factory activity contracting for a second month, with new orders falling at the fastest pace since late 2024, partly because of higher interest rates. The same survey showed input costs rising at the fastest rate since June and manufacturers raising selling prices more quickly.

For markets, the key question on Tuesday may be less whether the RBA hikes than how it frames the outlook. Guidance that keeps further increases on the table would support JPMorgan’s view of a persistent hawkish bias, while greater emphasis on slowing growth would suggest the cycle is nearing its peak.

TITRE : China’s central bank goes daily on 1.25% overnight repo, moving to Fed-style anchor
DATE  : 02-11-2026 01:44 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/china-s-central-bank-goes-daily-on-1-25-overnight-repo-moving-to-fed-style-anchor/
================================================================================

For China’s money markets, daily fixed-rate overnight operations should mean tighter control of short-term funding costs and less of the month-end and tax-season volatility that has periodically rattled interbank rates. The shift also changes what traders will watch: the overnight rate, not the seven-day rate, now looks set to become the signal of PBOC intent, so any future adjustment to the 1.25% level will be read as a policy move. WithOctober PMIs in contractionand equities coming off theirworst month in more than a decade, a cleaner transmission channel gives Beijing a sharper tool if it decides to lower borrowing costs. For the yuan, the change is structural rather than directional, but it modernises a framework global investors have long found opaque.

---

China is quietly swapping out the dial it uses to steer interest rates, trading a seven-day benchmark for an overnight one that brings its monetary toolkit closer to the Fed’s than ever before.

Summary:

China’s central bank is changing the way it sets the price of money. From Monday, the People’s Bank of China will run overnight reverse repo operations every working day at a fixed rate of 1.25%, a step that moves it closer to making the overnight rate, rather than its long-standing seven-day benchmark, its main policy anchor, Reuters reported.

The PBOC said on Friday that the daily operations would use a fixed-rate tender based on quantity, and that the aim was to improve its management of short-term interest rates. It was the first time the bank had disclosed the overnight operating rate. At 1.25%, it sits below the 1.4% seven-day reverse repo rate and is broadly in line with market expectations and actual borrowing costs.

The change is more than technical housekeeping. For years, the seven-day rate has been the reference point for Chinese monetary policy, with a cluster of other tools priced at a spread above or below it. Those tools, including temporary repo and reverse repo operations and repo facilities for overseas central bank-type institutions, will now be priced off the overnight rate instead, with the spreads unchanged. The seven-day operations will continue, but they move to a fixed-quantity tender with multiple winning bid rates and will be used mainly around tax payment periods and month-ends.

In other words, the overnight rate is becoming the centre of gravity. One economist described Friday’s announcement as arguably the clearest signal yet that the PBOC is shifting toward an overnight-rate operating system similar to those used by the Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank and the Bank of Japan. Another said the reform lays the groundwork for diversifying the benchmarks used to price loans and for passing broad-based rate cuts through the financial system more effectively.

As we noted earlier this month, the move had been signposted. PBOC Governor Pan Gongsheng told a financial forum in October that the bank was studying a switch of its main policy rate to the overnight reverse repo rate. The overnight operations themselves only debuted in late June, so the PBOC has moved from introducing the tool to building its framework around it in about four months.

The timing is notable. China’s economy is losing momentum, with official manufacturing and services surveys both slipping into contraction in October and stocks suffering their worst month in more than a decade. A clearer, more market-based anchor gives the PBOC finer control over funding costs, and a more direct way to transmit any easing it chooses to deliver.

DATE  : 02-11-2026 03:55 UTC

With a hike close to fully priced, the asymmetry is clear. A hold, as Deutsche Bank expects, would likely knock the Australian dollar lower and rally short-dated government bonds, while a hike would largely confirm what markets already expect. The Reserve Bank of New Zealand’s surprise hold last week shows that central banks can defy near-unanimous pricing, which may make traders less complacent going into the decision. The oil call matters too: Deutsche’s view that Brent falls to $90 by early next year contrasts sharply with Goldman Sachs’ new $105 December forecast, so the outlook for fuel-driven inflation, and therefore for the RBA, depends heavily on how the Middle East plays out after the US midterms.

---

Earlier:

---

On a day built around picking winners, Deutsche Bank has backed the outsider, betting the RBA ignores the crowd and holds rates while the oil outlook clears.

Summary:

Almost every economist and trader expects the Reserve Bank of Australia to raise interest rates on Melbourne Cup Day. Deutsche Bank is betting it won’t, the Australian Financial Review reported.

Of 32 economists surveyed by Reuters, 31 forecast a quarter-point increase on Tuesday that would lift the cash rate to 4.85%, its highest level in nearly two decades and the fifth hike this year. That group includes Australia’s big four banks. Markets price around an 85% chance of an increase, and many traders expect a hike before Christmas if it does not come this week. The decision is due at 2:30pm Sydney time (0330 GMT, 2230 US Eastern on Monday).

The lone dissenter among major forecasters is Phil O’Donoghue, Deutsche Bank’s chief economist for Australia, who expects the board to hold. His case is that markets are reacting to old news. Inflation picked up to an annual 3.7% in the September quarter, well above the RBA’s target, but O’Donoghue argues that the figure was in line with the central bank’s own forecasts. The RBA has warned since March that prices were running hot and has already raised rates by a full percentage point, taking them to their highest level in 15 years. Unemployment, meanwhile, has risen to 4.7%, earlier than expected.

With borrowing costs biting and the economy cooling, O’Donoghue believes the board can afford to wait for more data in December. By then, he said, there will be new information on fourth-quarter inflation and a clearer outlook for global oil prices after the US midterm elections. Deutsche Bank forecasts Brent crude falling to $90 a barrel by early next year. Fuel prices have risen about 60% since the war in the Middle East began eight months ago and have been a key driver of inflation.

O’Donoghue acknowledged that the RBA has a “free hit” to raise rates, simply because markets and the media have prepared everyone for one. But he said he hoped the board would reach its own conclusion rather than rely on the public “echo chamber”, noting that the Reserve Bank of New Zealand did exactly that last week when it held rates despite markets being fully priced for an increase.

He is not entirely alone in questioning the consensus. Another bank economist said markets had got ahead of themselves, putting the chance of a hike closer to 60% to 70%, and noted that the decision rests with a nine-member board after four hikes in eight months. Two fund managers have also tipped a pause, one citing the strength of the Australian dollar, near 70 US cents, and the other warning that inflation may have peaked and the economy risks a prolonged downturn.

The RBA has defied market expectations before. In July last year, it held rates when traders had priced a 95% chance of a cut. JPMorgan, among the many banks expecting a hike, has argued that senior officials have signalled inflation is not yet under control. Whether Tuesday brings a fifth increase or a surprise pause, the board’s guidance and its new forecasts will be closely watched for signs of how close the tightening cycle is to its peak.

TITRE : Market playbook: Can the RBA surprise markets again and give AUD/USD another reason to rally?
DATE  : 02-11-2026 07:58 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/market-playbook-can-the-rba-surprise-markets-again-and-give-aud-usd-another-reason-to-rally/
================================================================================

The aussie will be hoping that Christmas will come early this year with the RBA perhaps delivering a timely present tomorrow. But with markets already well expecting a rate hike this week, will Bullock have anything left in her bag of tricks to give the aussie another leg higher?

As things stand, traders are pricing in roughly 87% odds of a 25 bps rate hike tomorrow, which will take the cash rate to 4.85%. And this will follow the rate hike from September, with inflation proving to be a rather stubborn headache to deal with for policymakers.

But looking further out, traders are barely pricing in any further tightening beyond the one expected tomorrow. In fact, traders only see around 33 bps of cumulative rate hikes by June 2027 and that includes the expected move this week.

Essentially, that is signaling a one more and done move by the RBA.So, could the central bank catch traders off guard given the backdrop ahead of tomorrow?

I would argue that it's not outside of the realms of possibility as the central bank will want to retain as much flexibility and optionality as they can, as do all major central banks these days. Themore hawkish scenariowould be a 25 bps hike accompanied by stronger warnings on inflation and a clear indication that further tightening remains firmly on the table.

And if Bullock explicitly pushes back against expectations of an extended pause, that could force traders to rethink the rate outlook. If so, that would be the ideal recipe for another  push higher in AUD/USD.

But for that, we need to see three things happen. The first being the RBA signaling that inflation remains a serious concern. That means simply acknowledging that inflation pressures are stubborn  won't be enough.

Second, Bullock must challenge the idea that tomorrow's rate hike could be the last. And lastly, the RBA needs to sound sufficiently hawkish to shift the expected rate path.

It's not necessarily the hardest of hurdles to clear but to get through all of that is still rather challenging.

As for themore neutral scenario, it will just be the RBA delivering the rate hike as expected and then keeping their options open. That means Bullock reiterating that policy remains data-dependent without offering much encouragement for further tightening. And I suspect this will be the most likely outcome as she doesn't want to pre-commit to anything at this juncture.

Meanwhile, themore dovish scenariois where things could get the most uncomfortable for the aussie. Naturally, a surprise hold would be the most obvious shock. But I would say even a rate hike accompanied by softer inflation projections or hints that policy is now sufficiently restrictive could weigh on AUD/USD.

So, those are the three main scenarios to consider as we look to the decision tomorrow.

The question now isn't so much of whether the RBA chooses to raise the cash rate tomorrow. In all likelihood, they will deliver as expected. Instead, traders should be asking whether Bullock can convince markets that the tightening cycle still has another chapter to go. And that is where any real surprise opportunities will come for AUD/USD traders.
