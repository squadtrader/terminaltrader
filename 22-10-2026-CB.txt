TITRE : BoE preview: MUFG expects November rate hike as energy shock pushes UK inflation above 4%
DATE  : 21-10-2026 23:44 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/boe-preview-mufg-expects-november-rate-hike-as-energy-shock-pushes-uk-inflation-above-4/
================================================================================

A hike on November 5 would extend the energy-driven tightening seen across central banks. Any guidance pointing to a February follow-up would likely support sterling, particularly against currencies where central banks are seen as slower to respond. Gilts look the more vulnerable asset: 10-year UK yields already sit at the top of the G7 range, and further tightening alongside reduced fiscal headroom ahead of the Budget could keep pressure on longer-dated debt. For equities, higher rates and energy costs weigh on domestically focused UK stocks, while the FTSE 100’s energy-heavy constituents offer some offset. Oil and gas remain the key swing factor: with household energy bills driving the expected rise in inflation, any further spike in prices linked to the Middle East war would strengthen the case for a longer hiking cycle.

---

Earlier:

---

MUFG says the Bank of England cannot look through this energy shock the way it might like, because the UK economy is simply too strong to make waiting look safe.

Summary:

The Bank of England is likely to raise interest rates at its next meeting, as higher energy prices push UK inflation back above 4%, according to MUFG, which expects a further increase in February.

In its European macro and market outlook, MUFG said it expects a 25 basis point hike at the Bank of England’s next policy decision, due on November 5, followed by another in February, taking Bank Rate to a terminal level of 4.25%. The bank said energy prices have remained elevated for longer than anticipated and have moved higher in recent months. As those costs feed through to household bills, it expects headline inflation to rise above 4%.

MUFG argued that the Bank of England will struggle to look through the energy shock, as it might otherwise, because of the wider economic backdrop. The labour market is stabilising, growth is healthy and underlying inflation remains elevated. Services inflation measures are still running well above the Bank’s 2% target.

The UK economy has been notably resilient. MUFG said recent activity data have consistently surprised to the upside, and the economy does not appear to be repeating the second-half slowdowns of recent years, with third-quarter GDP tracking at about 0.65% quarter on quarter. It forecasts growth of 1.5% in 2026 and 1.4% in 2027, more optimistic than the IMF, despite energy-related headwinds.

That growth is fairly narrow, however, led by professional services and other knowledge-intensive sectors. MUFG said it is too early to draw firm conclusions, but the UK’s comparatively rapid adoption of artificial intelligence could help explain recent productivity gains. The share of UK job postings mentioning AI has risen faster than in the US or major European economies.

The fiscal backdrop is a complicating factor. MUFG estimates that higher borrowing costs may have reduced the government’s headroom against its fiscal rules to around £15 billion. It expects the upcoming Budget to include some offsetting tax measures alongside temporary, targeted support for households facing higher energy bills. MUFG questioned whether reducing fiscal buffers is prudent while growth holds up and geopolitical uncertainty remains high.

The Bank of England’s decision is due at 12:00 GMT (07:00 US Eastern time) on November 5.

TITRE : Bank of Korea holds rate at 3% after back-to-back hikes, keeps door open to November move
DATE  : 22-10-2026 01:31 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/bank-of-korea-holds-rate-at-3-after-back-to-back-hikes-keeps-door-open-to-november-move/
================================================================================

With the hold fully priced, attention shifts to Governor Shin’s news conference at 02:10 GMT for guidance on whether November remains live. A clear signal that the pause is temporary would likely support the won and push short-dated Korean government bond yields higher. A softer emphasis on monitoring the impact of past hikes could weigh on the currency. The board’s caution over recent stock market volatility suggests policymakers are wary of adding pressure to equities, which may offer some relief for the KOSPI. Chipmakers remain at the centre of the growth story given record AI-driven exports. Energy prices are a key upside risk to inflation for Korea as a major oil importer. That leaves the outlook sensitive to any escalation in the Middle East that lifts crude.

---

Earlier (preview):

---The Bank of Korea has stopped to catch its breath rather than end its tightening, with record chip exports and sticky inflation keeping a November hike firmly in view.

Summary:

The Bank of Korea left its benchmark interest rate unchanged on Thursday, pausing after back-to-back quarter-point hikes, though strong chip-driven growth and above-target inflation keep further tightening on the table.

The seven-member monetary policy board voted to hold the seven-day repurchase rate at 3.00%, Reuters reported, a decision forecast by all 34 analysts in its survey. The central bank raised rates in July and August, its first consecutive increases in more than three years. Governor Shin Hyun Song said afterward that a gradual pace of tightening was preferred.

Shin reiterated at an International Monetary Fund event on October 13 that policy will stay restrictive until inflation converges with the central bank’s target. Analysts said ahead of the meeting that a pause would slow the pace of tightening and soften its hawkish intensity. They noted the central bank had committed to monitoring the effects of its back-to-back hikes, and that recent market volatility, particularly in equities, made a hawkish decision less likely.

Headline consumer inflation slowed to 2.9% in September from 3.1% in August but remains above the Bank of Korea’s 2% target. Growth is being driven by exports, which are benefiting from global spending on artificial intelligence. Shipments for January to September exceeded $800 billion for the first time on record, underscoring the strength of South Korea’s semiconductor sector.

With growth expected to stay firm and inflation above target, the median forecast in the Reuters survey points to a hike to 3.25% at the Bank of Korea’s November meeting. That would be in line with the central bank’s own dot plot projection, which sets out policymakers’ expected path for rates.

The decision means the pause is likely to be read as a change in pace rather than direction. Markets will look to Shin’s news conference, due at 02:10 GMT, for signals on whether policymakers see a November hike as the most likely next step, and how they weigh firm growth and inflation against recent financial market volatility.
