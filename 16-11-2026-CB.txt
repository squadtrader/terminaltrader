TITRE : Sunday Fed talk: Schmid says inflation would still be near 3% without energy, Fed has work to do
DATE  : 15-11-2026 21:19 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/sunday-fed-talk-schmid-says-inflation-would-still-be-near-3-without-energy-fed-has-work-to-do/
================================================================================

Schmid’s remarks add weight to hike pricing that is alreadyin place for December. His attack on the “supply shock” argument also makes it harder to make the dovish case for looking through energy-driven inflation. That supports the US dollar and front-end Treasury yields, and it keeps the small tail risk of a half-point move alive. A Fed seen as committed to further tightening has been a headwind for gold throughout this cycle. His view that AI-driven demand will respond to higher rates is also a reminder to rate-sensitive tech valuations that the sector is not exempt from policy.

---

Schmid stopped short of calling for a hike, but he took apart both main arguments that inflation is beyond the Fed’s control, leaving little room for the doves ahead of December.

Summary:

Kansas City Federal Reserve President Jeff Schmid said on Sunday that US inflation is running too hot and that the Fed still has work to do to bring it under control. He pushed back against arguments that current price pressures are beyond the central bank’s reach.

Speaking to a credit union conference in Hawaii, Schmid said inflation is uncomfortably above the Fed’s 2% target and moving in the wrong direction, Reuters reported. He noted that inflation would still be running at about 3% even with energy prices stripped out, which suggests the problem is broader than the oil shock. He did not specifically call for further rate increases.

The core of his remarks was a rebuttal of two narratives that have gained ground recently. The first holds that high inflation reflects a run of supply shocks the Fed cannot fix. Schmid’s view is that inflation always comes from an imbalance between supply and demand, and that supply disruptions push prices higher precisely because demand is strong. Because the Fed influences demand, he argued, it always has both the tools and the responsibility to respond.

The second narrative holds that the current sources of demand, such as the artificial intelligence buildout, are resistant to higher interest rates. Schmid disagreed. He also rejected the idea that meeting the inflation goal requires prices in any single category, such as AI-related technology goods, to respond. In his view, it is not the Fed’s role to target the price of any one good or service.

Schmid described the labour market as broadly in balance, with little sign that jobs or wages are adding to inflation. He also echoed Chair Kevin Warsh, saying the work of containing inflation has only become harder since Warsh’s Jackson Hole speech.

Schmid is not a voting member of the policy-setting committee this year. The Fed raised rates by a quarter point in September to a 3.75% to 4.00% range, then held at its October meeting. Inflation data has since come in hotter than expected, and other officials, including Governor Christopher Waller, have also spoken. Futures markets are now fully priced for a quarter-point increase in December, with a small chance of a half-point move. Schmid’s comments add to the chorus arguing that the hiking cycle is not yet finished.

TITRE : Weekend Fed speak: Collins backs steady, gradual rate hikes, cool on a bigger December move
DATE  : 15-11-2026 21:34 UTC

================================================================================

Collins’s comments push back on the half-point talk that has built since Waller’s remarks and last week’s inflation data. That could trim some of the tail pricing for a larger December move and take a little heat out of the front end of the Treasury curve. Her view that the eventual rate path may be higher cuts the other way, though. It supports the idea of a longer hiking cycle, which keeps the US dollar supported on dips and leaves gold without a clear dovish tailwind. ---

Also:

---

Collins wants the Fed to keep hiking in small steps rather than speed up, but her admission that the destination may be higher means a gentler pace should not be confused with a softer stance.

Summary:

Boston Federal Reserve President Susan Collins said on Saturday she favours continuing to raise interest rates at a steady, modest pace, arguing that a gradual approach is the better way to handle the uncertainties facing the US economy, Reuters reported.

Speaking to reporters at a housing conference hosted by her bank in Boston, Collins said “a steady, gradual path” of tightening looked right to her for now, though she stressed she remained open-minded. She also cautioned that a gradual approach does not necessarily mean a set rhythm of moves.

Collins described the risks to the real economy as two-sided. On one side, inflation is high and price gains are spreading more widely through the economy, while the job market is stable and could pick up. On the other, the economy is still exposed to further shocks, and the rise in medium and longer-term borrowing costs since the start of the year may restrain demand more than she currently assumes.

Taken together, she said, those factors argue against the Fed stepping up to a more aggressive pace of increases, even though inflation has run above the 2% target for more than five years.

Her comments matter because the debate inside the Fed has shifted from whether to hike to how fast. The Fed last lifted rates in September and is widely expected to raise its 3.75% to 4% target range by a quarter point at the December 8-9 meeting, followed by another increase in January. Most officials have signalled support for small, steady moves, partly to give the economy time to absorb higher borrowing costs. However, recent inflation data and comments from Governor Christopher Waller have raised the possibility that some policymakers want a larger step next month.

Collins did not argue for less tightening overall. She said the past three months of price data, together with a firmer labour market, have led her to think the path of policy rates over the next year may end up somewhat higher than she expected a couple of months ago. Her preference is about pace rather than destination, which leaves the door open to more tightening, delivered in smaller steps.

TITRE : ECB’s Nagel says another rate hike ‘highly probable’ as energy prices push inflation
DATE  : 15-11-2026 21:51 UTC

================================================================================

An explicit signal from the Bundesbank president that further tightening is likely should support the euro and push up short-dated euro zone yields, especially German two-year debt. Because he ties the risk directly to gas and electricity, euro zone rate pricing is now more sensitive to moves in energy markets, so a rise in oil or gas could feed quickly into ECB hike expectations. His comment that the economy has proved resilient also weakens the argument that growth worries will hold the ECB back, which leaves rate-sensitive European equities more exposed.

---

Not only open mouths at the ECB:

----

Nagel has made it clear that the ECB is not finished: with energy prices running ahead of forecasts and expectations creeping higher, Germany’s central bank chief sees another hike as the likely price of avoiding a 2022 rerun.

Summary:

European Central Bank policymaker Joachim Nagel said on Friday that the ECB will very likely have to raise borrowing costs again, warning that energy-driven inflation pressures are building faster than the central bank had expected.

If conditions stay as they are, it seems “highly probable” that the ECB will need to act again, the Bundesbank president said in a speech, Bloomberg (gated) reported. He warned that each additional month of elevated inflation increases the risk of second-round effects, where higher prices feed into wages and broader price-setting.

Nagel said the inflation outlook has deteriorated since the ECB’s September projections. That baseline had inflation peaking around the turn of the year and staying above target well into 2027. Since then, he said, energy prices have moved well above the assumed path, and the economy is now approaching the adverse scenario set out in those projections.

He pointed to several sources of pressure. Gas and electricity prices have gathered momentum in recent weeks, and the early stages of the pricing chain point to further increases. Underlying inflation remains elevated, and short-term inflation expectations have risen alongside the jump in headline inflation.

Nagel also noted that the euro zone economy has so far proved resilient. That suggests he sees room to tighten without undermining growth. His remarks frame the policy question around the risk that an energy shock becomes entrenched, rather than around the strength of demand.

He offered some reassurance on the longer view, saying the Bundesbank’s analysis suggests a repeat of the 2022 inflation surge is unlikely, provided the ECB does its job. That condition matters. In his view, avoiding a 2022-style episode depends on the central bank responding in time, rather than waiting for energy prices to ease on their own.

As one of the more influential voices on the Governing Council, Nagel has added weight to expectations of further tightening. The next things to watch are energy prices, how much of them passes through to core inflation, and whether other policymakers share his view that the adverse scenario is becoming more likely.

TITRE : ECB bond market intervention warning from Governor Knot. Can’t replace sound fiscal policy,
DATE  : 15-11-2026 23:22 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/ecb-bond-market-intervention-warning-from-governor-knot-can-t-replace-sound-fiscal-policy/
================================================================================

Knot’s line on fiscal discipline matters most for euro zone sovereign spreads, especially French bonds against German Bunds. Markets will read it as a reminder that ECB support would be conditional rather than open-ended. A president with that view could make investors less willing to bet on a full central bank backstop if French finances get worse, which risks wider spreads during periods of stress. On rates, his cautious, data-driven stance on energy-driven inflation is softer than the hike signals coming from more hawkish policymakers, so a Knot presidency would lean toward patience rather than front-loaded tightening. The leadership race is now a live factor for the euro and for periphery bonds.

---

Knot is pitching himself as an ECB chief who will steady bond markets in a crisis but won’t bail out lax budgets, a message aimed at Paris as much as at the markets.

Summary:

The European Central Bank can step into government bond markets to restore order when trading turns disorderly, but it cannot take the place of responsible public spending and debt management. Former Dutch central bank governor Klaas Knot made the point as he campaigns to become the ECB’s next president.

His remarks carry particular weight because worries over France’s public finances have revived talk of a fresh euro zone debt crisis, putting a spotlight on how far the central bank would go to contain market stress. By drawing a clear line between short-term stabilisation and long-term fiscal discipline, Knot signalled that any ECB backstop would come with expectations for governments attached.

Knot is competing with former Spanish central bank governor Pablo Hernandez de Cos to succeed Christine Lagarde, who said last month she would step down on February 7, roughly nine months before her term ends. Whoever wins will inherit a structural challenge: 21 euro zone governments set their own fiscal policies, which makes coordination with a single monetary policy harder.

On the economy, Knot struck a broadly upbeat tone. He pointed to resilient domestic demand, a strong labour market and healthy private-sector balance sheets. He also highlighted higher defence spending and US investment in artificial intelligence as further sources of activity that benefit Europe.

On inflation, he urged caution. He acknowledged that the Iran war has pushed up energy prices and added to near-term inflation pressure. He said the key unknown is whether higher energy costs feed through into wages and wider consumer prices. With inflation expectations broadly stable and little evidence so far of widespread second-round effects, he backed a measured approach, saying policymakers should stay flexible and let incoming data guide future rate decisions.

Knot also said his thinking on monetary policy has changed since the earlier debt crisis, when persistently low inflation split northern and southern policymakers. That shift could matter for how governments in the south see him.

The next ECB president will have to balance price stability against the risk of financial instability, at a time when geopolitical tensions and government borrowing are clouding the euro zone outlook.

French bond spreads have only been one part of this story.

TITRE : BOJ’s Takata calls for nimble hikes after 1.5% bid, says neutral rate may top estimates
DATE  : 16-11-2026 04:06 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/boj-s-takata-calls-for-nimble-hikes-after-1-5-bid-says-neutral-rate-may-top-estimates/
================================================================================

Takata’s speech is the clearest public argument yet for a faster BOJ hiking pace than markets assume. He rejects a fixed quarterly rhythm and argues for reaching the middle of the neutral range early, which puts a move beyond 1.5% within view. Because his views are his own and he has been outvoted, it doesn’t change December pricing by itself. But it supports the yen and adds upward pressure on front-end JGB yields, especially with USD/JPY near 160. His explicit link between real rate differentials and the currency also signals that BOJ hawks see rate hikes as a tool against yen weakness. On the same day as a weak GDP print, the debate on the board is now plainly about speed, not direction.

---

Takata wants the BOJ to stop hiking on a timetable and start hiking on the data, and with producer prices running at 7% and the yen near 160, he thinks the data already argues for moving faster.

Summary:

Bank of Japan board member Hajime Takata said in a speech to business leaders in Tokushima on Monday that the central bank should raise interest rates nimbly rather than at a fixed pace. He argued that 2026 marks a regime change for Japan’s monetary policy and that the neutral rate may prove higher than markets expect.

Takata was the lone dissenter at the October meeting, when he proposed lifting the policy rate to 1.5% from 1.25%. In the speech he laid out his reasoning in detail, stressing that the views were his own. He said the BOJ should not be bound by particular intervals or ranges anticipated in markets, and should instead respond to domestic price and economic conditions as well as developments overseas.

At the heart of his argument is a shift in the inflation picture. Takata said underlying inflation has reached the 2% target and medium- to long-term inflation expectations have risen above 2%, increasing the risk of second-round effects. He flagged producer price inflation of 7.1% in October and faster pass-through of costs to consumers, and said energy costs driven by the Middle East conflict could push prices above target. As a result, he argued, the BOJ should shift from encouraging inflation towards showing markets its determination to prevent prices overshooting, while taking account of the effects of the currency.

He also pointed to the yen. Japan’s real policy rate remains the lowest in the world. With some currency market participants focusing on real rate differentials, he said, price pressures transmitted through the exchange rate are one reason hikes are needed now.

On the neutral rate, Takata said Japan’s policy rate has only just reached the neutral zone, which the BOJ estimates broadly at 1.1% to 2.5%. With central banks abroad raising rates, he warned that Japan’s neutral rate could end up higher than markets assume. He said the BOJ should move early to a level near the middle of that range, which would give it room to hike or cut later.

Takata said the cumulative effects of past hikes are still some way off. He cited strong corporate balance sheets, accommodative financial conditions, a recovering global economy and AI-related demand, which he expects to remain solid despite turbulence in stock markets in early November.

His remarks come on the same day that data showed Japan’s economy contracted slightly in the third quarter, with markets watching whether the BOJ moves in December.
