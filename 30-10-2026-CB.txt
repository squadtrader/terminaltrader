================================================================================

The yen is the most direct channel for a surprise. A firmer-than-expected reading would likely lift yen strength and push Japanese government bond yields higher as traders add to December hike bets. A softer print would tend to weigh on the yen and could support Japanese equities, particularly exporters. With the BoJ decision following within hours, any move on the data may be short-lived, or amplified, depending on the central bank’s tone.

-

Tokyo inflation is expected to ease only slightly, leaving it comfortably above target and keeping a December BoJ hike firmly in the conversation.

Summary:

Tokyo consumer price data for October lands just hours before the Bank of Japan announces its policy decision on Friday. It gives markets one last read on inflation as policymakers weigh the timing of their next rate hike.

Core inflation in the capital, which excludes volatile fresh food prices, is expected at 2.6% year on year, slightly below September’s 2.7%. Even at that level, the measure would remain above the central bank’s 2% target. The data are due at 23:30 GMT, or 8:30 am in Tokyo.

Two other measures will help markets judge the breadth of price pressures. Headline Tokyo inflation ran at 2.7% in September, while the gauge that strips out both food and energy stood at 3%. That second measure is often treated as a guide to underlying inflation because it removes the items most exposed to swings in commodity prices and weather. A reading that holds near 3% would suggest domestic price pressures remain firm, even if the core figure eases slightly.

The Tokyo numbers matter beyond the capital. They are published weeks ahead of the nationwide figures and are widely used as an early indicator of where national inflation is heading. That gives them extra weight when they land on the same day as a policy meeting.

The BoJ is widely expected to keep its policy rate at 1.25% later in the day, with attention focused on whether officials signal a move at the December meeting. A core reading at or above the 2.7% prior would likely support the case for tightening before the end of the year. A sharper drop than forecast could encourage expectations that the central bank will wait longer.

Labour market figures for September are released at the same time, with the jobless rate expected to hold at 2.5%. The BoJ’s quarterly Outlook Report, due at 03:00 GMT, will then show how the central bank’s own inflation forecasts compare with the latest data.

TITRE : BoJ preview: hold at 1.25% expected, with 2% inflation call and dissent risk in focus
DATE  : 29-10-2026 20:47 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/boj-preview-hold-at-1-25-expected-with-2-inflation-call-and-dissent-risk-in-focus/
================================================================================

The yen and short-end Japanese government bond yields are where the reaction will show. A clear 2% declaration, a firm Ueda and a hawkish dissent would likely support the yen, push two-year yields higher and add to December pricing. Dovish dissents calling for a cut could outweigh hawkish language and trigger renewed yen selling, a risk sharpened by the yen’s heavy losses earlier this year. Oil remains a key swing factor: further gains in crude, tied to the conflict in the Middle East, strengthen the case for faster tightening because energy feeds directly into Japan’s import costs and inflation outlook. USD/JPY is also still digesting this week’s Federal Reserve meeting.

Earlier:

---

The Bank of Japan looks set to stand still this week, but a formal nod to 2% inflation and a split board could do the moving for it.

Summary:

The Bank of Japan is widely expected to keep its policy rate at 1.25% when it concludes its two-day meeting on Friday. Markets are focused less on the decision than on whether the board signals a hike in December.

A move this week would be a major surprise. The central bank raised rates in June and again in September, taking its benchmark to the highest level in 31 years, and traders were pricing virtually no chance of back-to-back tightening heading into the meeting. A Nikkei report citing a senior official said consecutive hikes would only be considered in a serious emergency. Market pricing pointed to roughly a 73% chance of a further increase in December. A Reuters poll of 78 economists found 69% expect the rate to reach 1.50% this quarter, with most of those naming December.

The main event is likely to be the quarterly Outlook Report. Analysts broadly expect the board to effectively declare that underlying inflation has reached its 2% price stability target. That shift would move policy from lifting inflation toward keeping it stable. Governor Kazuo Ueda said earlier this month that, with underlying inflation close to 2%, the bank could not tolerate a further rise. A Reuters report citing sources familiar with the central bank’s thinking said policymakers may also nudge up their fiscal 2027 inflation forecast as higher oil prices feed through to consumer prices. Analysts at one Japanese brokerage expect upgrades to the growth and core inflation projections, driven mainly by crude.

The vote could be as important as the statement. Two board members opposed the September hike, and MUFG flagged the risk that they dissent against a hold and call for a cut. That would reinforce a dovish image and could trigger yen selling. Other analysts expect board member Hajime Takata to dissent in the opposite direction, proposing a hike as he did in July.

Ueda’s press conference will be watched for any change in language. If he moves away from saying rates are decided at every meeting toward phrasing that points to the next meeting, markets would likely read it as a strong December signal. A more guarded tone, focused on reviewing financial conditions after the September increase, could trim hike expectations.

There are reasons for caution. October surveys showed household and business inflation expectations easing, and sources told Reuters there is little evidence of a broader wage-price spiral. ING expects a hold now, followed by two hikes in 2027. Against that, several forces keep pressure on the central bank to continue tightening: oil-driven price pressure linked to the conflict in the Middle East, broadening cost pass-through to consumers and the risk of renewed yen weakness.

With the policy rate already inside the bank’s estimated neutral range of about 1% to 2.5%, the debate is shifting from whether the BoJ hikes again to how far it goes. Economists in the Reuters poll increasingly see a terminal rate of 2% or higher.

Bank of Japan Governor Ueda news conference due at 0630 GMT.

Capping expectations could reduce volatility further out the curve, while short-dated contracts stay sensitive to each inflation print. Deutsche Bank thinks the ECB has one more hike in it, and that the coming change of personnel matters less than the price of energy.

Summary:

Deutsche Bank expects the European Central Bank to raise interest rates one more time in December. It argues that the tightening already delivered may not be enough to contain inflation if energy prices keep rising.

The call follows Thursday’s ECB meeting, at which policymakers held all three key interest rates steady, leaving the deposit rate at 2.5%. Deutsche Bank had expected the pause, describing it as a measured response to the energy price shock. The euro and euro zone bond yields fell after the decision.

Mark Wall, chief European economist at Deutsche Bank Research, said the rate increases delivered so far are unlikely to be sufficient on their own if energy costs continue to feed through to consumer prices, even together with the tightening in financial conditions since September. The bank therefore still sees a final hike in December, a move it regards as the likely end of the current tightening cycle.

Wall also acknowledged that forecasting the ECB’s path has become more difficult, in part because of a wave of leadership changes at the central bank. President Christine Lagarde will step down in February, roughly nine months before her eight-year term was due to end in October 2027. She is only the second ECB president to leave early. Executive board member Isabel Schnabel is also departing a year early, in January, to take up a senior role at the International Monetary Fund. Chief economist Philip Lane’s term expires in May.

The race to succeed Lagarde is already under way. The Dutch government is backing former Dutch central bank governor Klaas Knot, while Spain supports Pablo Hernández de Cos, general manager of the Bank for International Settlements and a former Bank of Spain governor.

Wall questioned whether the leadership changes would alter decision-making. He argued that the ECB as an institution is bigger than any single president and that a new head does not automatically mean a different rate path.

If Deutsche Bank is right, the December move would set a ceiling on near-term rate expectations and shift the debate from how high rates go to how long they stay there. The new leadership’s tone in 2027 will then become the key question for markets.

ING likes the Aussie’s yield and expects the RBA to add to it next week, but sees the Fed standing between AUD/USD and 0.72.

Summary:

ING has raised its short-term forecast for the Australian dollar to 0.71 against the US dollar, citing the currency’s strong October and its attractive yield advantage. The bank does not expect AUD/USD to climb back to 0.72 this quarter, however, as a hawkish Federal Reserve keeps supporting the greenback.

The upgrade rests on ING’s view that the Reserve Bank of Australia will raise its cash rate by 25 basis points at its 3 November meeting, rather than waiting until December. That would lift the cash rate to 4.85%. The bank had previously pencilled in a hike at some point in the fourth quarter, and now sees the latest inflation data as strong enough to bring the move forward. Markets are pricing around 20 basis points of tightening for next week, slightly short of a full hike.

ING said AUD/USD is driven mainly by short-term interest rate differentials. It added that the Australian dollar currently offers the best short-term carry in the G10 once volatility is taken into account. Carry refers to the return investors earn from holding a higher-yielding currency, and a further RBA hike would widen that advantage.

The inflation backdrop supports the case. Headline consumer prices rose 4.3% from a year earlier in September, with inflation picking up by about 90 basis points between July and September, based on the latest monthly figures and the third-quarter report. Almost all of that increase came from transport costs, largely reflecting higher fuel prices. ING noted that goods and services inflation are both now running above 4%, a sign that energy costs are spreading more widely through the economy. Core inflation also accelerated on both annual and quarterly measures. The trimmed mean rose to 3.7%, above forecasts and well above the RBA’s target band.

ING concluded that underlying inflation is not on a sustained downward path, weakening any argument for a pause. At its last meeting, the RBA raised rates unanimously and signalled it was prepared to accept some softening in the labour market to bring inflation back to target.

The main limit on further Australian dollar gains lies outside Australia. With the Fed also leaning hawkish, ING expects the US dollar to remain supported, capping the upside for AUD/USD in the near term even if the RBA delivers next week.

================================================================================

A back-to-back hike would extend the Australian dollar’s rate advantage and keep front-end yields elevated, while any hint that November is the last move could trigger a pullback in both. Fuel is the key transmission channel, so oil prices remain central to the outlook: further crude gains would deepen the pass-through into services and strengthen calls for more tightening. Australian equities, particularly rate-sensitive sectors such as property and consumer discretionary, face added pressure if markets begin to price a longer hiking path.

Earlier:

---

Fuel is no longer just a petrol-station problem for Australia’s inflation, and Natixis expects the RBA to respond again next week.

Summary:

Australia’s September inflation data delivered a sharp upside surprise that is likely to push the Reserve Bank of Australia into a back-to-back rate hike at its meeting on Tuesday, 3 November. That is the view ofAlicia Garcia Herrero, chief economist for Asia Pacific at Natixis and one of the more closely followed analysts of the RBA.

Headline consumer prices rose about 0.8% in September from August and around 4.3% from a year earlier, both ahead of consensus forecasts of 0.7% and 4.2%. Garcia Herrero said the print surprised the market as well as the central bank.

The detail is what concerns her most. Energy added roughly a third of a percentage point to the monthly increase, but travel was the single largest driver, contributing around 0.43 percentage points, its biggest contribution since December 2025. Travel prices are closely tied to fuel costs, so Garcia Herrero said the data suggest the energy shock has begun to filter through to the wider basket of goods and services. She described this as a warning sign for policymakers.

Underlying inflation is also moving away from the path the RBA expected. The trimmed mean, the central bank’s preferred core measure, rose to about 3.7% year on year in the third quarter, edging up from the second quarter. In August, the RBA had projected that the trimmed mean would peak in the second quarter and ease back towards 3.3% by the end of 2026. The latest reading widens the gap between that forecast and the actual inflation path.

Garcia Herrero linked the data to recent comments from RBA officials. Deputy Governor Andrew Hauser recently described the inflation outlook as having deteriorated materially. Governor Michele Bullock told a parliamentary hearing this week that the central bank will not tolerate inflation remaining persistently high. Against that backdrop, Garcia Herrero expects the RBA to favour another hike next week.

Beyond November, she expects policy to become more finely balanced. The RBA is likely to keep a hawkish tone while inflation remains elevated. However, signs of an easing labour market and slowing household spending under higher interest rates mean subsequent decisions are likely to be highly data dependent.
