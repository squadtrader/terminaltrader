DATE  : 22-10-2026 22:37 UTC

The French-German 10-year spread is the key gauge: MUFG’s scenarios put it at about 170 basis points in its base case and above 200 in a crisis, levels last associated with the euro zone debt crisis. EUR/USD has been tracking the two-year swap spread between the euro and the dollar closely, so any further scaling back of ECB hike expectations is likely to weigh on the currency, while a firm December signal would offer some support. In bonds, heavy redemptions from the ECB’s portfolio in coming months put the pace of quantitative tightening in focus. Any hint of a slowdown could ease pressure on French debt. Energy remains a swing factor: MUFG’s forecasts assume supply risks fade, so persistently high oil and gas prices tied to the Middle East conflict would challenge both its rate path and its call for a gradual euro recovery.

---

Earlier:

---

MUFG expects the ECB to hold next week as higher bond yields and French fiscal stress tighten conditions for it, but still sees two more hikes, with France the biggest risk to the euro.

Summary:

The European Central Bank is set to keep rates on hold at next week’s policy meeting, but MUFG argues the tightening cycle is not over, even as rising bond yields and French fiscal stress do some of the work for policymakers.

In its October European outlook, MUFG said the bond market sell-off and growing fiscal risks in France have taken an October rate hike off the table. ECB officials have increasingly pointed to the impact of tighter financial conditions on the outlook, and underlying inflation pressures remain contained. Even so, the bank’s strategists argue that resilient economic activity and higher gas prices since September still justify a move into slightly restrictive territory as a risk-management measure. MUFG expects a 25 basis point hike in December and another in the first quarter of 2027, taking the policy rate to 3.00%. It then sees the ECB cutting back to 2.75% by the third quarter of 2027, a path that assumes energy supply risks fade.

France is at the centre of the outlook. MUFG notes that a fragmented parliament has led successive governments to miss fiscal targets repeatedly, leaving the deficit close to 6% of GDP despite several consolidation plans. With public debt around 120% of GDP, the bank says France has little room for further slippage and is highly exposed to weaker growth or a rise in borrowing costs. The IMF’s latest forecasts already show France as the clear outlier among major European economies, with growth seen at just 0.4% this year.

The strain is visible in the bond market. The spread between French and German 10-year yields has widened to levels last seen during the euro zone debt crisis, while France’s annual interest bill has climbed to a record. MUFG suggests that a high and rising share of French debt held by foreign investors may have amplified the sell-off. Contagion has so far been limited, with Italian spreads relatively contained.

The bank sets out four scenarios ahead of France’s presidential election, in which it describes Marine Le Pen as the frontrunner. Its base case, given 45% probability, is continued drift: the government survives by relying on procedural mechanisms, measures are diluted or delayed, and the deficit stays between 5.6% and 6.0% of GDP. In that case, MUFG sees the French-German spread around 170 basis points and EUR/USD in a 1.08 to 1.14 range. It would not force an immediate ECB response, but quantitative tightening could come under review.

A deeper credibility crisis, at 15%, would bring acute political instability, a recession and a deficit well above 6% in 2027. That would push the spread above 200 basis points and drive EUR/USD down to between 1.02 and 1.10, with investors looking to the ECB’s Transmission Protection Instrument as a potential backstop. On the more positive side, limited consolidation (35%) would leave the spread near 110 basis points, while faster consolidation (5%) would narrow it to about 90 basis points and support EUR/USD in a 1.12 to 1.18 range.

For the currency, MUFG says the scaling back of ECB hike expectations has already helped weaken the euro, which has closely tracked short-term rate differentials with the US. The bank expects the dollar to remain stronger for longer. It forecasts EUR/USD at 1.12 at the end of this year before a gradual recovery to 1.14 in the first quarter of 2027, 1.16 in the second and 1.18 in the third.

With policymakers in their quiet period, next week’s statement and press conference will be watched for how much weight the ECB gives to tighter financial conditions and French fiscal risks, and whether it keeps the door open to a December move.

TITRE : Japan’s 2.4% underlying inflation puts a higher BoJ terminal rate in play
DATE  : 23-10-2026 01:27 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/japan-s-2-4-underlying-inflation-puts-a-higher-boj-terminal-rate-in-play/
================================================================================

The debate is shifting from when the BoJ hikes again to where rates peak, which matters most for the front end of the JGB curve. A market that moves from pricing a terminal rate around 1.75% to one closer to or above 2% would be likely to push two-year and five-year yields higher after their recent rally. The yen firmed after the data, but a sustained rally would probably need the BoJ to endorse a higher peak next week, given the large trade deficit and the wide gap with US rates. For equities, a higher-for-longer BoJ path tends to favour banks over exporters. Oil remains the key upstream driver: Middle East-linked energy costs are feeding Japanese inflation even with fuel subsidies in place, so crude above $110 a barrel keeps upside risk on both prices and the BoJ’s forecasts.

---

Earlier:

---

Japan’s inflation is now broad enough that a December BoJ hike looks like the starting point rather than the question, with markets turning to how far the bank will ultimately need to go.

Summary:

Japan’s underlying inflation accelerated in September, with price increases broadening across goods and services despite government fuel subsidies. The data strengthen expectations of a Bank of Japan rate hike in December and are shifting attention to how high rates may ultimately go.

Core consumer prices, which exclude fresh food but include fuel, rose 2.1% from a year earlier, matching forecasts and accelerating from 1.7% in August. It was the first reading above the BoJ’s 2% target in nine months, Reuters reported. The index excluding both fresh food and fuel, which the BoJ watches closely as a better measure of demand-driven inflation, rose 2.4% from 1.9%.

The breakdown showed price pressure spreading. Goods inflation rose to 2.8% from 2.6%, while services inflation increased to 1.5% from 1.2%, suggesting firms are starting to pass higher wages on to customers. Rising fuel costs linked to the prolonged Middle East conflict, higher import prices from a weak yen and strong demand linked to artificial intelligence have together blunted the impact of the fuel subsidies.

Services inflation remains the weak link in that picture. At 1.5%, it is still well short of 2%, a reminder that the wage-driven, domestically generated inflation the BoJ has long sought is building only gradually.

The BoJ is widely expected to keep its policy rate at 1.25% at its two-day meeting ending October 30, having raised it to a 31-year high in September. Sources have told Reuters the bank may revise up its inflation forecasts, signal that underlying inflation has likely reached 2%, and stress its focus on anchoring inflation at that level. Governor Kazuo Ueda said last week that the BoJ would not tolerate an inflation overshoot.

One economist told Reuters that rising energy costs and their knock-on effects should push core inflation higher, but that prices are not accelerating sharply enough to justify back-to-back hikes, and he expects the next move in December. Many analysts polled by Reuters expect the BoJ to raise rates to 1.50% in December, with a terminal rate of around 1.75% to 2%. Some see the risks tilting higher. Capital Economics said there is little evidence that tightening so far has slowed the economy and that the policy rate may peak above its 2% forecast. Recent surveys have shown little sign that higher rates are weighing on loan demand.

Not all of Friday’s data supported a more aggressive path. A flash business survey released the same morning showed private sector growth steady but subdued in October, with job creation slowing to a 14-month low and services firms cutting staff for the first time in more than a year. Economy Minister Minoru Kiuchi said conditions have not yet been met for the government to declare an official end to deflation, and he urged the BoJ to guide policy appropriately.

That leaves the BoJ facing a familiar balance at next week’s meeting: inflation that is finally broadening, against signs that the labour market and domestic demand are losing some momentum. How the bank describes that balance in its outlook report is likely to shape market expectations for the policy rate well beyond December.
