TITRE : Moody’s Zandi says a Fed hike this week would be a mistake as 5% yields do the tightening
DATE  : 27-10-2026 00:27 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/moody-s-zandi-says-a-fed-hike-this-week-would-be-a-mistake-as-5-yields-do-the-tightening/
================================================================================

Zandi’s view runs against market pricing for at least one more hike this year, so it’s more a marker for the doves than a market mover. It does sharpen focus on the tension in this week’s Fed decision. Oil is the pivot: crude climbing back toward wartime peaks on Iran tensions lifts inflation and keeps the Fed on its hiking path, while also squeezing real incomes in a way that strengthens Zandi’s growth warning. With 10-year yields near 5% and credit spreads wider, any hawkish surprise from the Fed risks further pressure on rate-sensitive sectors such as housing and leveraged borrowers. A skip with hawkish guidance, as Goldman Sachs expects, would keep December live.

---

Earlier:

---

Zandi’s case is simple: the bond market has already tightened for the Fed, and hiking into an oil shock risks doing the economy’s real damage for it.

Summary:

Moody’s Analytics chief economist Mark Zandi has warned that raising interest rates at this week’s Federal Reserve meeting would be a mistake, arguing that the bond market is already doing the tightening for the central bank and that a policy error now ranks among the growing risks facing the US economy.

Writing on X on Monday, Zandi pointed to the sharp rise in borrowing costs since the Fed’s September hike. The 10-year Treasury yield is hovering around 5%, fixed mortgage rates are above 7% and corporate bond spreads have widened. In his view, that rise in market rates is already restraining the economy, which leaves no need for the Fed to add to it. The FOMC meets on October 27 and 28, with the federal funds rate at 3.75% to 4.00% after September’s quarter-point increase, and markets pricing at least one more hike before year-end.

Oil sits at the centre of his argument. With prices climbing back toward their wartime peaks on tensions with Iran, Zandi said higher energy costs will push up inflation, but warned that rate hikes cannot fix a supply problem. Instead, he argued, higher oil prices will erode real wages and consumer spending, and tightening into that squeeze risks tipping the economy into trouble.

Zandi also pointed to Moody’s risk matrix, which ranks threats to the economy over the next 12 to 18 months by likelihood and potential damage. He said it contains a lot of red, with risks including an AI stock sell-off, a Fed misstep, intensifying wars, trade escalation and financial repression.

The warning is consistent with his earlier stance. Zandi described September’s hike as a serious mistake and warned in late September that higher rates were already damaging the economy. The Fed’s case for tightening rests on inflation that remains well above target, with headline PCE estimated at 3.8% in August and core at 3.4%, alongside price pressure from oil, tariffs and AI investment.

Some on Wall Street expect the Fed to wait this week. Goldman Sachs sees a skip in October followed by a hike in December, which would leave Zandi’s argument as much a test for the December meeting as for this one.

TITRE : Japan services PPI picks up to 3.7% as August revised lower, backing BoJ hike path
DATE  : 27-10-2026 01:20 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/japan-services-ppi-picks-up-to-3-7-as-august-revised-lower-backing-boj-hike-path/
================================================================================

The downward revision to August makes the September print more hawkish than the headline suggests: services prices are accelerating, not just holding. That adds weight to the case for the BoJ’s Outlook Report to effectively declare underlying inflation has reached 2%, and supports December hike pricing, lending the yen modest support into Friday. Short-end JGB yields could edge higher as markets price a firmer BoJ tone. Oil-driven cost pressure from the Iran conflict remains a background inflation risk, but this data shows domestic, labour-driven pressure building too. The vote split and Ueda’s press conference remain the bigger yen catalysts.

---

Earlier:

---A quiet downward revision to August turned Japan’s in-line services print into an acceleration, and that’s the version of the story the BoJ will be reading this week.

Summary:

Japan’s services producer price index rose 3.7% year on year in September, in line with expectations and faster than a downwardly revised 3.5% gain in August, Bank of Japan data showed on Tuesday. The pick-up points to mounting service-sector price pressure ahead of a BoJ meeting this week that is expected to leave rates on hold while signalling further tightening ahead.

The revision changes the picture. August’s reading was initially reported at 3.7%, which had suggested September would merely match the prior month. With August now at 3.5%, the September figure marks an acceleration rather than a steady pace, a more hawkish read for policymakers watching whether inflation is becoming entrenched in the domestic economy.

The index tracks the prices companies charge each other for services such as transport, communications, finance, real estate, advertising and equipment leasing. The BoJ watches it closely for evidence of how far firms are passing rising labour costs on to service prices, a central part of its case that wage growth and inflation are reinforcing each other. A faster pace suggests that pass-through is gathering momentum rather than fading.

The data contrast with the goods side, where producer price inflation slowed to 6.8% in September from 7.6% in August as fuel subsidies eased some raw-material costs. Services prices rising while goods inflation slows suggests domestic, labour-driven pressure is taking over from imported costs as the main driver.

The BoJ concludes a two-day meeting on Friday and is expected to keep its policy rate at 1.25%, after raising it in September, while signalling it is ready to continue lifting borrowing costs as price pressures build. Most economists expect the next hike in December. The key focus is whether the bank’s updated Outlook Report effectively declares that underlying inflation has reached its 2% target, a step it has never taken since adopting the goal in 2013.

Today’s acceleration strengthens the case for that declaration and makes it harder for the board members who opposed September’s hike to argue for easier policy.
