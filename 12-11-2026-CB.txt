TITRE : Japan wholesale inflation speeds up to 7.1%, beating forecasts and firming BoJ hike bets
DATE  : 12-11-2026 00:01 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/japan-wholesale-inflation-speeds-up-to-7-1-beating-forecasts-and-firming-boj-hike-bets/
================================================================================

The upside surprise adds to the case for a Bank of Japan hike in December. It should give the yen some support at the margin, although aFed that is also on a hiking path after a hot US CPIlimits how much the rate gap narrows. Oil is the key swing factor, because crude and fuel imports drove most of October’s import price jump. Any further rise in energy prices tied to the Iran war would feed directly into Japan’s pipeline inflation and strengthen the hawks’ argument. For Japanese government bonds, a firmer inflation backdrop keeps upward pressure on yields. For equities, rising input costs are a risk to margins for manufacturers that cannot pass them on.

---

Cheap rice is masking it, but energy, materials and a weak yen are pushing Japan’s pipeline inflation higher again, and that leaves little reason for the BoJ to wait.

Summary:

Japan’s wholesale inflation picked up again in October, as producer prices rose faster than forecast and added weight to expectations that the Bank of Japan will raise interest rates in December. The corporate goods price index rose 7.1% from a year earlier, Bank of Japan data showed on Thursday. That was above the 6.5% median forecast and up from 6.8% in September. On the month, prices climbed 0.7%, more than double the 0.3% expected, after a revised 0.2% decline in September.

The rebound ends two months of slowing in the annual rate, and it was driven mainly by energy and industrial materials. On the month, petroleum and coal products rose about 3%, chemicals about 2%, iron and steel 2%, and electricity, gas and water around 2.4%. Together, those four groups added roughly 0.6 percentage point to the monthly increase.

The headline would have been even stronger without food. Agricultural products fell 6.6% on the month and are down a quarter from a year ago, as cheaper newly harvested rice continues to drag on the index. Without that drag, the underlying push from manufactured goods and energy was close to a 1% monthly rise. That suggests pipeline pressure is spreading, not fading.

Import costs remain the main engine. The yen-based import price index jumped 3.3% in October and is nearly 25% higher than a year ago. Most of the monthly gain came from crude oil and other fuels, with energy imports up about 46% on the year in yen terms, as prices stay elevated during the Iran war. A 1% slide in the yen over the month added to the pressure. Imported memory chips and other electronics also rose sharply, echoing the firmness in technology goods prices seen elsewhere.

For the Bank of Japan, the data support a move at its December meeting. The central bank held its policy rate at 1.25% in late October in an 8-1 vote, with board member Hajime Takata pushing for an immediate hike. It also said underlying inflation had reached levels broadly consistent with its 2% target. A Bloomberg survey published before that meeting showed 83% of economists expecting a December increase.

Producer prices are an upstream gauge, and the BoJ places more weight on consumer inflation and wages. But with a weak yen and high oil prices, these costs are likely to keep feeding through to households, which makes a pause harder to justify.

TITRE : BoJ’s Asada, who opposed 2 hikes this year, urges caution on pace as rates near neutral
DATE  : 12-11-2026 01:38 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/boj-s-asada-who-opposed-2-hikes-this-year-urges-caution-on-pace-as-rates-near-neutral/
================================================================================

Asada’s views are well known, so the speech is unlikely to change December pricing much. A December hike is still the consensus, and a single dissent would not block it. The timing matters for the yen, though. A dovish voice from the board, on the same morning thatofficials are warning about rapid moveswith USD/JPY near 160, gives yen bears a talking point and makes Tokyo’s verbal defense harder. For Japanese government bonds, his emphasis on caution supports the view that any hikes beyond December will be gradual, which could cap yields at the front end. With oil-driven input costs still pushing producer prices up, though, the hawks on the board hold the stronger hand for now.

---

Asada isn’t trying to stop a December hike so much as put the brakes on what comes after it, arguing that with rates near neutral, every further move carries more risk.

Summary:

Bank of Japan board member Toichiro Asada urged caution on the pace of further interest rate increases on Thursday. He argued that with policy already inside the estimated neutral range, each additional hike carries a growing risk of slowing the economy. The remarks, in a speech to local leaders in Nagasaki, mark him once again as one of the board’s most dovish voices ahead of the December meeting, where most economists expect a hike.

Asada voted against both of this year’s rate increases, in June and September, which took the policy rate to 1.25%, the highest in around three decades. He said the Bank estimates the neutral interest rate, the level that neither stimulates nor restrains the economy, at roughly slightly above 1% to 2.5%. With the policy rate already in that range, he argued that monetary accommodation is gradually diminishing and that further hikes are more likely to hurt activity than when the tightening cycle began. He added that it can take a year or more for the effect of a rate increase to become clear.

His central concern is consumption. The Bank officially describes private spending as resilient, but Asada said it has been more or less flat and is not strong enough to drive overall growth. In his view, recent price gains have not necessarily been driven by demand. He said any further hike would most likely come when higher wages spur demand-driven inflation, and that the Bank should give due consideration to the pace of tightening.

He did acknowledge strength elsewhere. Corporate profits are high, supported by global AI-related demand. Unemployment is around 2.5%, base pay rises have held up for a third year, and real wages have been positive since the start of 2026. He also noted that producer price inflation has been running at around 7%, and said the key question is how quickly cost pressures further up the supply chain reach consumers. Core consumer inflation is around 2%, held down by government energy subsidies, although a measure that excludes such institutional factors is running at 3% to 3.5%.

Asada’s speech came on the same morning that data showed Japan’s producer prices rising a faster than expected 7.1% in October. Finance ministry officials were also warning against rapid yen moves, with USD/JPY near 160. Those pressures strengthen the case for the majority on the board. A single dissent would not block a December hike, but it would underline that the board’s appetite for a faster pace of tightening is limited.

TITRE : Deutsche Bank sees 3 straight Fed hikes after hot CPI, adding a January move
DATE  : 12-11-2026 02:54 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/deutsche-bank-sees-3-straight-fed-hikes-after-hot-cpi-adding-a-january-move/
================================================================================

A no-pause path into March is more hawkish than markets have priced beyond December. If other banks follow, it would likely be repriced through the front end of the Treasury curve and the dollar. That keeps USD/JPY pressed toward the 160 area whereTokyo is already warning, and it adds to the headwinds for gold. For equities, a faster sequence of hikes lands at an awkward moment, with AI stocks already under pressure from theNvidia leadership shock. Oil remains the wildcard: renewed energy-driven inflation would strengthen the case for back-to-back hikes, while a sustained easing in crude would weaken it.

---

Deutsche Bank has turned UBS’s “faster tightening” risk into its base case, and the Fed debate is now about the pace after December, not whether December happens.

Summary:

Deutsche Bank now expects the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates at each of its next three meetings, after adding a January increase to its forecast following a hotter than expected US inflation report. The bank sees quarter-point hikes in December, January and March. That would lift the federal funds target range to 4.50% to 4.75%, from 3.75% to 4% now.

Deutsche Bank had previously expected hikes in December and March, with a pause in January. The October consumer price report changed that view. Headline prices rose 0.4% on the month against forecasts of 0.3%, and the bank said the data continued to show broad-based gains.

The bank also addressed the idea of a larger move. Before the data, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a 50 basis point hike should be considered in December if price pressures accelerated, and Deutsche Bank said his comments opened that possibility. It would not rule out a half-point increase, but said a 50 basis point move is not its base case.

The revision puts Deutsche Bank at the more hawkish end of the major banks. UBS, for example, said this week it sees limited support on the rate-setting committee for a 50 basis point step. It keeps a base case of quarter-point hikes in December and March only, although it flagged the risk of more or faster tightening. The two views share a common thread: the debate has shifted from whether the Fed hikes in December to how quickly it follows through.

Markets are close to fully pricing a December move. Traders put the odds of a quarter-point hike at the December 8-9 meeting above 95%, according to CME FedWatch.

The path matters well beyond bonds. Three consecutive hikes would keep pressure on Treasury yields, which climbed close to 5% after the CPI release, and would support the dollar. It also raises the bar for assets that have struggled with higher rates, from gold, which has slipped back below $4,000 an ounce, to richly valued technology stocks. The next tests come from November’s labor market data and further Fed commentary before the December meeting.

TITRE : ANZ says RBA hike bets have cooled, but 3.7% core inflation will keep it cautious
DATE  : 12-11-2026 03:02 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/anz-says-rba-hike-bets-have-cooled-but-3-7-core-inflation-will-keep-it-cautious/
================================================================================

Cooler RBA hike expectations take away one source of support for the Australian dollar, at a time when US yields are rising and theFed is on a hiking path. That leaves the AUD exposed to further dollar strength. Sticky services inflation should limit how far Australian rate expectations can fall, though, which caps the downside for front-end bond yields. Lower oil prices help Australian headline inflation but do little for the domestic services pressures ANZ is focused on, so the RBA’s cautious stance looks likely to persist.

---

Growth is cooling, but services inflation isn’t, and that keeps the RBA from relaxing even as the case for another hike fades.

Summary:

Expectations of further interest rate increases by the Reserve Bank of Australia have cooled as signs mount that the economy is slowing. But persistent inflation means the central bank is unlikely to drop its guard, according to ANZ economist Siddhant Kalra.

The pressure shows up most clearly in the RBA’s preferred core measure. The trimmed mean, which strips out the most extreme price moves each quarter, rose 1.0% in the third quarter and 3.7% from a year earlier. That was above forecasts and above the top of the central bank’s 2% to 3% target band. Market services inflation, which covers prices that depend mainly on local demand and labor costs, posted its strongest annual increase since 2024.

Kalra highlighted the services detail as the key point. Market services prices are closely linked to domestic capacity pressures, so they reflect how stretched the Australian economy is, rather than global influences such as oil prices or the currency. A strong reading suggests demand is still running up against the economy’s limits, which is the kind of inflation central banks find hardest to bring down.

ANZ said the strength of the result is likely to keep the RBA cautious in the coming months. That leaves the bank in an awkward position. Slowing growth argues against more tightening, while sticky underlying inflation argues against signaling that rates have peaked, let alone opening the door to cuts.

The global backdrop adds to the tension. Lower oil prices are easing pressure on headline inflation, but central banks elsewhere are leaning hawkish. The US Federal Reserve is widely expected to raise rates in December after a hotter than expected October inflation report, and the Bank of Japan is also expected to hike.

For the RBA, the coming months are likely to be about patience rather than action. Unless services inflation eases, a meaningful shift toward lower rates looks some way off, even if the bar for another hike has risen.
