Four hikes in, the RBA’s forecasting chief says the job is not finished, with war-driven oil costs working their way into supply chains and a fifth increase looking likely on November 3.

Summary:

Reserve Bank of Australia Assistant Governor Sarah Hunter said the central bank has not yet finished its fight against inflation, reinforcing expectations that policymakers will raise interest rates again when they meet on November 3.

Speaking in an interview with Nine Network’s 60 Minutes, broadcast on Sunday evening Australian time, Hunter noted that the board had delivered four rate increases this year to tackle persistent price pressures. Asked whether that tightening had been enough to tame inflation, she replied “not quite yet.” She declined to front-run the board’s next decision, but stressed that policymakers remained committed to bringing inflation down.

Her remarks build on comments from Deputy Governor Andrew Hauser on Wednesday, when he said the inflation outlook had become materially worse since the bank’s August assessment. Those remarks had already lifted market expectations for a hike at the November meeting.

Hunter, who oversees economic forecasting at the RBA, pointed to the ongoing US-Iran war as a source of renewed pressure. Higher oil prices linked to the conflict are beginning to become embedded in some supply chains, she said, adding that the bank expects more of that pass-through to come. The comment suggests the RBA is increasingly concerned that the energy shock is spreading beyond fuel prices into the broader cost base of the economy, which would make inflation more persistent and harder to bring back down.

The RBA lifted its cash rate in late September to 4.6%, the highest level in 15 years. A further increase would add downward pressure on Australian property prices, which have already been falling. Hunter said the decline in house prices was not, so far, creating systemic financial stress across the system, a signal that the bank does not see housing weakness as a reason to hold off.

Attention now turns to Wednesday, when Australia’s third-quarter consumer price data are released. The trimmed mean measure of underlying inflation is expected at 3.6%, broadly in line with the previous quarter. Governor Michele Bullock and other policymakers are also scheduled to appear before parliament the same day, giving them another opportunity to set out their thinking ahead of the decision. A reading in line with forecasts, combined with the oil-driven pressure Hunter described, would likely leave a November hike as the market’s base case.
