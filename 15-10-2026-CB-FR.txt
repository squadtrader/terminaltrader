TITRE : BOJ’s Koeda says bank should not be overly cautious about raising rates
DATE  : 15-10-2026 01:47 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/boj-s-koeda-says-bank-should-not-be-overly-cautious-about-raising-rates/
================================================================================

Koeda’s message leans hawkish: by placing the neutral rate considerably above the current 1.25% policy rate and warning against excessive caution, she keeps further BOJ hikes firmly on the table. That should offer the yen some support, particularly against a backdrop where Fed hike expectations have also risen, which narrows the room for the yen to weaken on rate differentials alone. Her observation that the yen has lost some of its safe-haven role is notable for currency traders, since it suggests geopolitical shocks may now hurt the yen through higher oil import costs rather than help it. Japanese government bond yields may also face upward pressure if markets price a faster path toward neutral.

-

Koeda’s message to markets is that 1.25% is not the destination, and the BOJ shouldn’t let caution slow the trip.

Summary:

Bank of Japan policy board member Junko Koeda said on Thursday that the central bank should not become overly cautious about raising interest rates, pointing to considerable upside risks to prices and financial conditions that remain accommodative.

In a speech to local leaders in Kumamoto, Koeda said the BOJ should adjust its policy rate in a timely manner while keeping in mind the estimated range of the neutral interest rate, the level at which policy neither stimulates nor restrains the economy. She said the neutral rate is likely considerably above 1%, which she described as roughly the bottom of the estimated range, and that one estimate based on potential growth could put it above 2%. The BOJ’s own estimates imply a nominal neutral rate of around 1.1% to 2.5%. Following the BOJ’s rate hike in September, the overnight call rate stands at around 1.25%, leaving room for further increases on her reasoning.

Koeda said underlying inflation appears to be becoming anchored at around 2%, in line with the BOJ’s price stability target. More than 70% of the over 500 items in Japan’s consumer price index are rising year on year, and the distribution of price changes has shifted higher, she said. With wages also increasing steadily, sustained inflation appears to be taking hold more than before.

She highlighted several sources of upside risk to prices. Geopolitical shocks, including the situation in the Middle East since the spring, have repeatedly driven up crude oil and commodity prices. Koeda also noted that the yen has been less reliable as a safe haven in recent crises, continuing to weaken during this year’s Middle East escalation as oil surged, which has pushed up import costs. Strong global demand linked to artificial intelligence is adding further inflationary pressure.

On the economy, Koeda described recent indicators as solid overall, citing high corporate profits, moderate growth in business investment and continued wage gains. She said rising rates were not expected to significantly reduce household consumption, and that firms’ funding conditions remained favourable.

Koeda also stressed the need to keep normalising the BOJ’s balance sheet, saying its substantial size should not be underestimated. The BOJ is reducing its monthly government bond purchases in stages and plans to hold them at about 2 trillion yen a month from April 2027.

TITRE : AUD lower: Australian jobs fall 39,300 in September, unemployment at five-year high of 4.7%
DATE  : 15-10-2026 02:06 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/aud-lower-australian-jobs-fall-39-300-in-september-unemployment-at-five-year-high-of-4-7/
================================================================================

The report shifts the RBA debate from when the next hike comes to whether it comes at all, with a pause now looking the likelier near-term outcome. That leaves the Australian dollar out of step with the wider market. While gold and major currencies have risen against the US dollar on lower Treasury yields, the Aussie has stayed near its post-data lows, as a narrowing rate outlook at home offsets the broader dollar weakness. Further AUD underperformance is likely on crosses such as AUD/NZD and AUD/JPY if markets keep paring RBA hike bets. The noisy nature of the data means the next release, and the RBA’s revised forecasts in November, will carry extra weight.

--Australia’s jobs market blinked in September, and with it went most of the market’s appetite for a November RBA hike.

Summary:

Australian employment unexpectedly fell in September, while the unemployment rate held at a five-year high of 4.7%, prompting traders to scale back bets on another Reserve Bank of Australia rate hike.

Data from the Australian Bureau of Statistics on Thursday showed employment dropped by 39,300 in the month, well short of forecasts for a gain of around 20,000. August’s increase was revised down to 29,300. The decline was driven entirely by part-time work, which slumped by 73,700, its weakest month in five years, while full-time employment rose by 34,400.

The unemployment rate was steady at 4.7%, above expectations of 4.6%. August’s rate was revised up to 4.7% from 4.6%, although only just: unrounded, the jobless rate was 4.72% in September against 4.65% in August. A sharp drop in the participation rate to 66.8% from 67.1%, below the 67.0% expected, limited the rise in unemployment, as fewer people were looking for work. Hours worked rose 0.1% and underemployment edged up to 6.3%.

The ABS urged caution in interpreting the seasonally adjusted figures, citing ongoing volatility in the data. That volatility comes at an awkward time for the RBA, which relies on the labour figures to judge how tighter policy is affecting the economy. The split between full-time and part-time jobs, and the swing in participation, have both been noisy from month to month.

Even so, the trend is softening. The unemployment rate averaged 4.6% over the quarter, well above the RBA’s forecast, which is likely to force the bank to revise its projections higher at its November meeting. With the RBA operating under a dual mandate of price stability and full employment, a weaker jobs market makes the risks around further tightening more balanced.

Markets moved quickly to reflect that. Pricing for a November RBA rate hike fell and markets no longer favour a hike before the first half of 2027. Australian three-year government bond yields fell circa 10 basis points.

The Australian dollar dropped on the release and has stayed near its lows, standing out against gains in gold and other major currencies as US Treasury yields have fallen.

TITRE : ECB’s Santos Pereira says euro zone inflation is not spreading beyond energy
DATE  : 15-10-2026 02:27 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/ecb-s-santos-pereira-says-euro-zone-inflation-is-not-spreading-beyond-energy/
================================================================================

The comments support the view that the ECB can hold its nerve on an energy-led price shock, which should limit pressure for tighter policy in the near term and leave the euro without a fresh rate-driven boost. His attribution of higher yields to fiscal issues rather than inflation is notable for bond traders, since it suggests the ECB sees the selloff as a matter for governments rather than for monetary policy. That points to limited appetite to lean against yields through policy. Natural gas is the swing factor, and a cold start to winter would quickly test his relaxed stance and could revive hawkish pricing.

Álvaro Santos Pereira is the governor of the Banco de Portugal, Portugal’s central bank. That makes him a member of the ECB’s Governing Council.

The ECB can live with an energy shock as long as it stays an energy shock, and so far, says Santos Pereira, it has.

Summary:

Energy-driven inflation in the euro zone is not spreading to the wider economy, European Central Bank policymaker Alvaro Santos Pereira said, offering a reassuring signal for the bank as it weighs the risk of higher prices.

Speaking at the Bangkok Economic Festival, the Portuguese central bank governor said the good news was that inflation in Europe had not broadened so far. He pointed to the share of goods and services with prices rising by more than 3% or 4%, which he said had stayed broadly stable over roughly the past two years.

Santos Pereira also made clear where the line sits. If inflation does start to broaden, he said, central banks will be forced to act. That suggests the ECB is prepared to look through an energy shock for now, but would respond if price increases began to spread into other categories.

He flagged natural gas as the main risk. A winter like last year’s would create a problem for gas prices, he said, which would feed directly into energy costs for households and businesses. He also said the recent rise in bond yields was more closely tied to fiscal concerns than to the inflation outlook.

The measure he cited is a breadth or diffusion gauge, which tracks how widespread price increases are rather than how fast prices are rising on average. It is gaining attention among central bankers. Bloomberg Economics highlighted a similar measure for the United States this week, showing fewer US consumer price categories running hot in September. Bank of Japan board member Junko Koeda pointed to the more than 70% of Japanese CPI items rising year on year as evidence that inflation there is taking hold.

For the ECB, the readings to watch are the next euro zone inflation figures, the path of natural gas prices as winter approaches, and whether the share of fast-rising prices starts to climb.

TITRE : RBNZ’s Conway says October rate decision completely open, OCR more likely to rise
DATE  : 15-10-2026 02:57 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/rbnz-s-conway-says-october-rate-decision-completely-open-ocr-more-likely-to-rise/
================================================================================

Conway’s comments keep a late-October hike live, which should support the New Zealand dollar against currencies where rate expectations are falling, most notably the Australian dollar after Thursday’s weak jobs report. That divergence makes AUD/NZD a natural expression of the gap between a dovish-leaning RBA and a still-hawkish RBNZ. By declining to commit, the RBNZ leaves markets highly sensitive to the next round of New Zealand data, so expect pricing to swing between a hold and a hike before the decision. A firm tightening bias also limits the scope for New Zealand yields to fall much further in the near term.

-The RBNZ wants the market to know a hike is coming, just not necessarily when.

Summary:

The Reserve Bank of New Zealand’s next interest rate decision in late October is “completely open”, chief economist Paul Conway said, while repeating that the central bank still sees the official cash rate as more likely to rise than fall.

Speaking on the sidelines of the IMF and World Bank meetings in Bangkok, Conway said the bank wants to keep its options open between holding the rate steady and raising it by 25 basis points. The RBNZ continues to see the next move in the OCR as most likely to be higher, he said, but it has not decided on timing.

Conway said recent moves in oil prices and the New Zealand dollar have added to inflation pressure in the short term. Even so, the bank remains confident that inflation will slow back into its 1% to 3% target band next year.

The comments leave the October decision finely balanced. By describing the meeting as completely open, Conway avoided pre-committing the bank to a hike, while his reaffirmation of a tightening bias makes clear that a cut is not on the table. That gives the RBNZ room to respond to incoming data, including inflation figures, before the announcement.

The RBNZ’s stance stands out against shifts elsewhere in the region. Across the Tasman, softer Australian jobs data on Thursday prompted traders to cut the odds of a November Reserve Bank of Australia hike to around 15%. In the United States, a hotter inflation report has pushed markets to price a strong chance of a Federal Reserve hike in December.

The key question for the late-October meeting is whether short-term price pressures from energy and the currency prove strong enough to justify acting now, or whether the bank’s confidence in inflation returning to target next year allows it to wait a little longer.
