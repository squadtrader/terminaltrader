DATE  : 23-11-2026 22:58 UTC

================================================================================

The bigger story for currency markets is the policy divergence. The RBA is now widely expected to start easing in mid-2027, while the Fed is still on a hiking path, with a December increase broadly expected. A narrowing rate gap between the two would weigh on the Australian dollar against the US dollar over time. Short-dated Australian rates may see limited reaction, because markets had already priced a June cut as more likely than not after the jobs data. The effect is more likely to show up further out on the curve. Any upside surprise in quarterly inflation would test ANZ's call quickly and could prompt a sharp repricing back toward a later start to easing.

---

Yesterday:

---After five hikes this year, ANZ thinks the RBA will reverse course by mid-2027, though that depends on inflation cooling faster than the central bank itself expects.

Summary:

ANZ now expects the Reserve Bank of Australia to begin cutting interest rates in June 2027, five months earlier than its previous forecast of November 2027, making it the first of Australia's big four banks to pencil in relief before the second half of next year.

In a research note released on Tuesday, ANZ Head of Australian Economics Adam Boyton set out a new easing path. The bank now sees a 25 basis point cut in June 2027, taking the cash rate from 4.85% to 4.60%, followed by a second cut in August 2027 to 4.35% and a third in February 2028 to 4.10%. Under its previous forecast, ANZ expected only two cuts, in November 2027 and February 2028.

The change comes less than a month after the RBA delivered its fifth rate increase of 2026 on November 3, lifting the cash rate to 4.85%, its highest level since 2008. The other three major banks currently expect the first cut in August 2027.

Boyton said three conditions need to play out over the next six months to justify a cut in the first half of next year: further deterioration in the labour market, an unwinding of excess demand across the economy and a moderation in underlying inflation. He said there is already early evidence pointing toward each of those developments.

The labour market is the most visible of the three. Employment fell sharply for a second straight month in October, pushing the unemployment rate up to 4.9%, a five-year high. That is above 4.75%, the RBA's estimate of the jobless rate consistent with stable inflation, a threshold that matters because it suggests the jobs market is no longer adding to price pressure.

Inflation is the harder test. The RBA's own forecasts, released after its November meeting, show inflation staying above 3% over the coming year and not returning to the top of its 2% to 3% target band until late 2027. For a June cut to happen, underlying price growth would need to slow faster than the central bank currently expects, or the RBA would need to be convinced that a weakening economy will finish the job.

ANZ's call also brings it closer to market pricing rather than ahead of it. After last week's jobs data, markets put the odds of a quarter-point cut at the June 2027 meeting at around 68%, rising to about 90% for August. Because the forecast still implies an extended hold from here, its main effect is on pricing further out along the yield curve rather than on expectations for the next few RBA meetings.

The key risks to the view run in both directions. A rebound in hiring or stubborn services inflation could push cuts back toward the second half of 2027, while a sharper slowdown could bring them forward again. Quarterly inflation and monthly jobs data over the next six months will decide which way the call breaks.

TITRE : Fed's Williams won't rule out back-to-back rate hikes as data drives the path
DATE  : 23-11-2026 23:08 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/fed-s-williams-won-t-rule-out-back-to-back-rate-hikes-as-data-drives-the-path/
================================================================================

The openness to consecutive hikes adds upside risk to Fed pricing beyond December, with traders more likely to build in a follow-up move in early 2027. That should keep short-dated Treasury yields supported and give the US dollar a firmer floor, especially against currencies whose central banks are moving toward cuts. For equities, Williams set a high bar for market weakness to change his view, which limits hopes that renewed tech volatility would slow the Fed. Energy remains central to his inflation outlook, so any rise in oil prices would add weight to the case for a faster pace of hikes.

---

Earlier:

---

Williams' message to reporters was simple: December may only be the start, and only a much deeper slide in market confidence than the recent tech sell-off would make him rethink.

Summary:

New York Federal Reserve President John Williams said on Monday he would not rule out raising interest rates at consecutive meetings if needed, adding a sharper edge to a day of remarks in which he stressed that inflation remains too high.

Speaking to reporters after his speech at the University of Mount Saint Vincent in New York City, Williams said he has no particular timing in mind for further rate increases and has not pencilled in a path for policy. Incoming data will drive what the Fed does, he said, and officials will have to learn along the way. But he was clear that the range of outcomes is wide. "I'm not excluding options," Williams said, adding that he would not rule out moving at back-to-back meetings if that was what was needed. He also said the Fed will respond if inflation pressures do not ease.

That matters for how markets read the December 8-9 meeting. Investors broadly expect a quarter-point increase from the current 3.75% to 4.00% range, with more to come next year. The Fed last raised rates in September, so a December move would follow a pause. Williams' comments suggest a December hike need not be followed by a long wait, and that the pace of tightening could pick up if inflation data disappoint.

Williams also addressed the recent turbulence in stocks. He said he was not surprised by the market volatility and that swings in the tech sector have not upended his positive outlook for the economy. He did leave one caveat: if market sentiment darkened a lot, that could affect his view of the economy.

That caveat sets a high bar. The S&P 500 had slid more than 10% from its October record before Monday's Nvidia-led rebound, and Williams still described the outlook as positive. Read that way, only a deeper and more lasting drop in confidence, one that starts to hit spending and hiring, would be likely to make him rethink the case for tighter policy. Ordinary market swings, even sharp ones, appear unlikely to do so.

His press comments rounded out a consistent message across the day. In his prepared remarks, Williams said inflation is too high, risks are skewed to the upside and the Fed still has work to do, while forecasting inflation at 3.5% at the end of this year and back at the 2% target in 2028. In the question-and-answer session, he struck a more measured tone, describing recent inflation as driven by unusual factors and calling 2027 a year of lower inflation. His remarks to reporters made clear that the measured tone should not be mistaken for a reluctance to act.

The next tests are the economic data releases due before the December meeting. Hot readings would strengthen the case for a faster pace of hikes into early 2027, while softer data would give the Fed room to move more gradually.

TITRE : Bank of Korea seen hiking to 3.25% this week, with one final move in early 2027
DATE  : 24-11-2026 00:46 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/bank-of-korea-seen-hiking-to-3-25-this-week-with-one-final-move-in-early-2027/
================================================================================

With a hike fully priced, the reaction on Thursday will depend on the BOK's guidance. A signal that the next move is the last would support the 3.50% peak view and could weigh lightly on the won, while an open-ended tone would push Korean bond yields higher. The won's 7% gain this year gives the BOK room to stay measured, but a run of Fed hikes would test that. Korean equities, already at 10-month lows after last week's tech sell-off, face the double hit of higher borrowing costs and fragile sentiment, though the semiconductor export story remains supportive for chipmakers.

---

Korea's chip boom is forcing the BOK's hand this week, but a fragile stock market and softening consumers suggest the hiking cycle is close to its end.

Summary:

The Bank of Korea is expected to raise interest rates on Thursday and follow up with one final increase early next year, according to a Reuters poll of economists, as a semiconductor-driven export boom keeps growth strong and inflation above target.

All but two of 34 economists surveyed between November 17 and 23 expect the central bank to lift its base rate by 25 basis points to 3.25% at its November 26 meeting. The remaining two forecast no change. The BOK has already raised rates by a total of 50 basis points in the current cycle. Looking further ahead, 18 of 28 economists see the base rate at 3.50% by the end of the first quarter of 2027, and the poll median shows the bank holding at that level for the rest of next year.

Why a hike looks locked in

The case for tightening rests on strong growth and sticky prices. Consumer inflation eased to 2.8% in October from 2.9% in September but stayed above the BOK's 2% target. Semiconductor exports nearly tripled in the first three quarters of the year from a year earlier, and third-quarter GDP came in much stronger than expected. Rising home prices add to the pressure.

An economist at Societe Generale said a hike is fully priced in by markets, so a decision to hold would come as a surprise. With inflation not expected to return to 2% until the second half of 2027, and with house prices still rising and chip exports robust, she said the case for another hike remains intact.

Why the cycle may end soon

Economists also see limits to how far the BOK can go. Domestic demand is weakening, and the Kospi fell to a 10-month low last week in a sharp technology sell-off, raising concerns that a hit to household wealth could weigh on consumer spending. That reinforces expectations that next quarter's move will be the last.

The currency is another factor. The won has gained about 7% so far this year, making it one of Asia's best-performing currencies, which reduces any need for the BOK to raise rates simply to support it. A separate Reuters poll earlier this month forecast the won strengthening a further 2.2% to around 1,320 per dollar by the end of April 2027.

The Fed factor

US policy could still push the BOK further. The Federal Reserve is expected to raise rates again in December, according to a separate Reuters poll, and a wider gap between Korean and US rates could weigh on the won and encourage capital outflows. One economist warned that if the BOK holds off while the Fed hikes, the reaction in currency markets would be hard to predict.

That link matters because Korea's base rate already sits well below the Fed's 3.75% to 4.00% range. By moving alongside the Fed this week, the BOK would stop that gap from widening further. If the Fed delivers more than one hike, a possibility New York Fed President John Williams left open on Monday when he said he would not rule out moves at consecutive meetings, pressure on Seoul to match it in early 2027 would grow.

The decision on Thursday will be judged as much on the BOK's guidance as on the hike itself. Any signal that the bank sees the next move as its last would support the market view of a 3.50% peak, while a more open-ended tone could revive talk of a higher terminal rate.

TITRE : Singapore sees AI boom carrying 2026, but slower AI investment growth to weigh on 2027
DATE  : 24-11-2026 00:52 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/singapore-sees-ai-boom-carrying-2026-but-slower-ai-investment-growth-to-weigh-on-2027/
================================================================================

Coming from one of the region's biggest AI winners, the comments are an early official signal that growth in AI capital spending may peak in 2026. That matters for chip and electronics supply chains across Taiwan, Korea and Japan. Markets are likely to read it as a slowdown in the rate of growth rather than a contraction, so there's limited near-term risk for AI-linked equities. Still, it adds to the debate over 2027 earnings expectations for the sector. For the Singapore dollar, firm near-term growth supports theMAS keeping its current settings in January, while the softer 2027 outlook limits the case for any further tightening.

---

Singapore's message is that the AI boom isn't ending, it's just growing more slowly, and for an economy this exposed to it, that alone is enough to roughly halve growth next year.

Summary:

Singapore's growth outlook for the rest of 2026 remains firm, with strong momentum in global AI investment set to continue through the end of the year, a senior trade ministry official said on Tuesday. But he also explained why the government expects growth to slow sharply in 2027: the pace of new AI investment is set to ease from this year's high, and growth in most of Singapore's key trading partners is expected to moderate.

Beh Swan Gin, permanent secretary at the Ministry of Energy, Trade and Industry, made the comments after the government released stronger-than-expected third-quarter figures. The economy grew 6.1% from a year earlier in the July to September period, well above the 5.4% advance estimate, and the ministry raised its 2026 growth forecast to around 6% from a range of 4.5% to 5.5%. For 2027, it expects growth of only 2.5% to 4.5%.

Beh's remarks fill in the reasoning behind that gap. He said growth in most key trading partners, including the United States and China, is expected to moderate next year, which would weigh on demand for a trade-dependent economy like Singapore. At the same time, he expects the pace of growth in new AI investment to ease from its 2026 high.

That distinction matters. Slower growth in AI investment is not the same as a fall. Spending can stay at very high levels while still adding less to growth each year, simply because it is rising from a much bigger base. For Singapore, whose electronics exporters have been among the biggest winners from the AI build-out, even a moderation in the rate of increase is enough to pull headline growth down. Enterprise Singapore's forecasts point the same way, with non-oil domestic exports expected to jump 30% to 32% this year before slowing to 5% to 7% growth in 2027.

The comments echo concerns elsewhere in the region. In Japan, business optimism in S&P Global's November flash PMI survey fell to its joint-lowest since 2020, with firms citing worries about a slowdown in AI-related investment among their concerns. Global banks have taken a broader view; Goldman Sachs recently estimated that $10 trillion to $15 trillion could flow into AI infrastructure over the next six years, with spending spreading across memory, packaging and power.

For now, the near-term picture for Singapore looks solid. The question for 2027 is whether AI spending slows gradually, as officials expect, or more abruptly, which would hit the city-state's export engine harder. The Monetary Authority of Singapore's January policy review will be the next chance to see how policymakers weigh strong current growth against a softer outlook.

TITRE : BlackRock says markets overprice Fed hikes, sees two not three as energy shock fades
DATE  : 24-11-2026 01:21 UTC
URL   : https://investinglive.com/central-banks/blackrock-says-markets-overprice-fed-hikes-sees-two-not-three-as-energy-shock-fades/
================================================================================

BlackRock's view runs againstMonday's hawkish Fed commentaryand against market pricing, so the October PCE data becomes a key test. A softer services reading would back BlackRock and could pull Fed pricing toward two hikes, supporting short-dated Treasuries and easing some pressure on the US dollar. For oil, BlackRock's case depends on the recent sharp fall in crude and fuel prices holding. A renewed rise in energy costs would undercut its view and strengthen the case for more Fed tightening. Its expectation of an ECB hike next month, with more depending on energy pass-through, points to tighter policy in Europe than the US for longer.

-

BlackRock thinks markets are pricing a Fed that will keep reacting to today's energy shock, when that shock is already reversing and will soon be pulling inflation down.

Summary:

BlackRock believes markets have gone too far in pricing Federal Reserve rate hikes, arguing that a fading energy shock will bring headline US inflation down next year and allow the Fed to tighten less than investors expect.

In its weekly market commentary, the world's largest asset manager said it agrees with market expectations for a quarter-point Fed hike in December. But with pricing pointing to as many as three quarter-point increases over the next 12 months, BlackRock said that is overdone. It currently expects just one more hike in the first half of 2027.

The energy shock has peaked

At the centre of BlackRock's view is the energy shock from the Middle East conflict. It estimates the surge in energy prices added 1.5 percentage points to headline US inflation at its September peak. With Brent crude, gasoline and diesel prices all falling sharply this month, it expects that contribution to reverse, turning from a boost to inflation this year into a drag of 0.8 percentage points by the end of 2027. Lower fuel costs should also ease core inflation through cheaper transportation services.

BlackRock expects pressure from tariffs and the AI build-out to fade next year as well, though unevenly. It added that tighter financial conditions and uncertainty around AI spending could temper demand and hiring in 2027, further limiting price pressures.

Why the Fed still hikes

BlackRock is not calling for an early end to tightening. The US economy is strong, the labour market is tight and wage growth of around 4% sits well above the 2% to 2.5% pace it sees as consistent with the Fed's target. That keeps core services inflation elevated, and BlackRock expects core inflation to stay closer to 3% than to the Fed's 2% goal. It also said the neutral interest rate may be higher than previously assumed, supporting further tightening even as energy relief arrives.

The contrast with Fed commentary is notable. On the same day, New York Fed President John Williams said he would not rule out raising rates at consecutive meetings if needed, and that the Fed would respond if inflation pressures do not ease. BlackRock's view implies a slower pace than that, with the October PCE inflation data, and especially services prices, a key test of which camp is right.

Beyond the Fed

BlackRock also expects the European Central Bank to raise rates next month. It said the outlook beyond December depends on whether energy-driven price pressures continue feeding into underlying inflation, noting that earlier rises in natural gas prices are still reaching European consumers, prolonging inflation there relative to the US. The US, as a net energy exporter, is better placed to absorb the shock.

On growth, BlackRock sees the US expanding 2% to 2.5% this year and moderating but staying above 2% in 2027 as AI spending grows more slowly and the boost from tax cuts fades.

What it means for portfolios

With two-year and 10-year Treasury yields reaching multi-decade highs this year, BlackRock said much of the long-anticipated structural rise in rates is now reflected in bond prices. It favours short- and medium-term Treasuries for income and protection against rate swings, and has strengthened its strategic overweight in inflation-linked bonds as a hedge against supply-driven inflation.

The commentary was written before Monday's sharp rebound in US stocks, after a week in which BlackRock noted solid Nvidia earnings and Anthropic's record IPO failed to lift AI sentiment and the S&P 500 fell into correction territory. If BlackRock is right that the market is over-pricing hikes, a repricing toward a gentler Fed path would favour short-dated bonds and ease some pressure on rate-sensitive equities.
